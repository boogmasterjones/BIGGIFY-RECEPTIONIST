Hiring a marketing company

30 questions to ask any marketing company before you sign

Locked into a contract that renews itself. A website you paid for that the agency "owns". A phone number on your trucks that rings someone else after you leave. These are the stories in the 1-star reviews, over and over. Every one of them could have been caught with one question in the first meeting. Here are those questions, what a good answer sounds like, and the answers that should end the meeting.

12 min read Checked against current Google and FTC rules By Wyatt, Biggify

You'll walk away with

  • 30 questions in 9 groups, each with a good answer and a red flag
  • The 6 answers that should end the meeting on the spot
  • A top-10 tick-off list to print and bring to the meeting

The 20-second version

Jump to the top 10 →
  1. Know how to leave before you sign. End date, renewal, cancellation fee, and how to cancel, all in writing.
  2. Everything with your name on it stays yours. Domain, website, Google profile, Facebook page, ads account, phone numbers.
  3. Count calls and jobs, not "impressions". Ask to hear the recorded calls they count as leads.
  4. See your ad spend separately from their fee. On a Google bill you can log in and read.
  5. Never sign in the first meeting. Take the contract home. Real deals are still there tomorrow.

01

What the 1-star reviews say

Read the BBB complaints and Trustpilot reviews for the big trade and small-business marketing companies and the same few stories keep coming back. None of them are about bad luck. They're about what was in the contract, or what wasn't asked.

  • "I couldn't get out." Contracts of 6 or 12 months that renew on their own. Cancellation only by phone, or only inside a short window before the renewal date. Billed for month 7 of a 6-month deal because the window was missed.
  • "They kept my website." The site was built on the agency's own system, so leaving meant getting some files and content back, and paying someone to rebuild it.
  • "My old number rings their system." The agency put its own tracking number on the Google profile and directories. When the owner left, the number went with the agency.
  • "The reports looked great. The phone didn't ring." Lead counts that included wrong numbers, spam and existing customers. Charts of "impressions" instead of calls.
  • "The price changed." A low number on the sales call, then setup fees, add-ons and a different total on the first invoice.
  • "Where did my ad money go?" One monthly bill that mixed the ad budget with the agency's fee, so the owner never knew how much Google actually got.
It's on paper, or it doesn't exist

Most complaints start with "the salesperson told me…". If a promise matters to you, ask for it to be written into the contract before you sign. A good company won't mind.

02

Contract and leaving

Ask these first. If the answers are bad, you don't need the other 25.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
1. How long is the contract, and what's the exact end date?A clear length and an end date you can point to. If it's long, a reason you can see, like a website build."It's standard, don't worry about it." Or a term that's only on page 9.
2. Does it renew on its own? For how long?No, or it rolls to month-to-month, and they remind you in writing before it does.Renews for another full 6 or 12 months unless you cancel inside a short window.
3. How do I cancel, exactly?An email or written notice to an address in the contract. They confirm it in writing."Call our retention team." Phone-only cancellation, with no written confirmation.
4. What does it cost to leave early?Nothing, or a fixed amount written in the contract, often tied to unpaid setup work."The rest of the term is due." Or a fee they can't tell you today.
5. What do I get handed back on the way out, and how fast?A list: domain, website files, logins, phone numbers, ad accounts, photos. A number of days."We'll work something out." Or the site "comes down" when you stop paying.
The law may not protect you here

Many state auto-renewal laws were written to protect consumers, not businesses. When you sign as a business, assume the contract is what counts. Put the cancellation date in your phone calendar the day you sign.

03

Who owns what

The test is simple: if they vanished tomorrow, could you log in to everything yourself?

QuestionGood answerRed flag
6. Whose name is the domain (my web address) registered in?Yours, on a registrar account you can log in to. They can be added to help."We register it for you" on their account, in their name.
7. Who is the Primary Owner of my Google Business Profile?You, on an email you control. They're added as a Manager.They want to be the Owner, or they'll "create a new profile" instead of using yours.
8. Who owns the Facebook page, Google Ads, Local Services Ads and Analytics accounts?You do. They get partner or manager access, which you can remove.The accounts live inside their agency account and you just get reports.
9. Do I own the photos, videos and written content you make for me?Yes, and you get the files when you leave."That's our licensed content." Your own job photos shouldn't be anyone else's.

04

What exactly you get each month

"Full digital marketing" means nothing. Get a list you could check off at the end of the month.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
10. What do I get each month, in numbers?Counts you can check: posts, pages, review replies, reports, calls handled."We do whatever it takes." "Ongoing optimization."
11. What's the total I'll pay in the first 3 months, all in?One number, including setup, add-ons and any ad budget, written on the quote.A low monthly price, then setup, "platform", "tracking" and "management" fees on the first invoice.
12. Who actually does the work, and who do I call?A named person who knows your business, and how fast they answer.A support ticket line and a new "account manager" every few months.
13. Is this price only good today?"Take it home and read it. The price is the same next week.""This discount ends when I hang up." That's pressure, not a deal.
05

How results are measured: calls, not "impressions"

Impressions, clicks and "visibility scores" don't pay your bills. Booked jobs do. Push every answer back to the phone.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
14. What number will you report to me every month?Calls and form leads from new customers, by source. Ideally booked jobs too, if you tell them which ones booked.Impressions, reach, "visibility", rankings for words nobody searches.
15. What counts as a "lead"?A real person asking about work you do. Spam, wrong numbers, vendors and existing customers are taken out."Every call is a lead." A 6-second hang-up is not a lead.
16. Can I listen to the calls you counted?Yes. Recordings or a call log with times and numbers you can check against your own phone."That's proprietary." Or only a total with no detail.
17. What will you promise, and what happens if it doesn't happen?Honest ranges, what depends on you (answering the phone, reviews), and what they'll change if the numbers are flat."Page one guaranteed" or "#1 on Google". Nobody controls Google's rankings.
Your own 2-minute check

Once a month, pick five "leads" from their report and look them up in your own phone's call history. If you can't find them, or they're your mother-in-law, you know what the report is worth.

06

Reviews and Google's rules

Some review tricks get you more stars this month and a suspended profile next month. Google bans fake and paid-for reviews, and so does the FTC, with fines that can reach the business that benefits.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
18. How will you get me more reviews?Asking every real customer, by text or card, right after the job. Same link for happy and unhappy customers."We have a network." Bought reviews, swapped reviews, or reviews written for you.
19. Do you only send the review link to happy customers?No. Everyone gets the same ask. Filtering out unhappy customers first ("review gating") breaks Google's rules."Unhappy ones go to a private form, happy ones go to Google."
20. Who writes the replies to my reviews, and in whose voice?Written as your business, in plain words, checked with you on anything negative.Copy-paste replies, arguing with customers, or offering discounts for changing a review.
07

Phones and tracking numbers

Tracking numbers are useful. They tell you which marketing makes the phone ring. The trap is who owns them.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
21. Whose name is the tracking number in, and is it mine if I leave?In your name, or written in the contract that it's transferred (ported) to you when you leave."It's on our system." Then the number on your trucks and listings leaves with them.
22. Where will you put the tracking number?On Google as the main phone with your real number kept as an "additional phone", and only where it's needed.Replacing your real number everywhere online. That creates mismatched listings that are slow to clean up.
23. When a call comes in, who answers it?Your phone rings first. Anything that answers for you passes the caller's details to you right away.Calls go to their call center or a "lead" you pay for, and you get a message later.
08

Websites

QuestionGood answerRed flag
24. If I leave, do I get a working website or just files?A site you can move to any host, or the full code and content, handed over in a set number of days.It's built on their own system, so you get "static files" and have to pay someone to rebuild it.
25. Can I see three sites you built for my trade, and call those owners?Yes, live sites, with the owner's number.Only screenshots, or sites that all look the same with the name swapped.
26. Is a website included, or do I pay for it twice?A clear price for the build and for hosting and updates, shown separately."Free website" that's only free while you stay on the monthly plan.

Some big companies are open about this in their own FAQs: you own the site after the contract term, but not the system it runs on, so moving means a rebuild. That's not hidden. It's just something to know before you sign, not after.

09

Ad spend vs their fee

If you pay one monthly number for "ads", you can't tell how much went to Google and how much was kept.

QuestionGood answerRed flag
27. How much of my monthly bill goes to Google (or Facebook), and how much is your fee?Two separate numbers. The ad spend is billed to your card by Google, or shown on Google's own invoices.One combined "budget". Or "we buy media at a discount", with no way to check.
28. Can I log in to the ads account myself?Yes, with admin access, from day one."We'll send you a report." You should be able to see the spend in Google, not just in their PDF.
10

References

QuestionGood answerRed flag
29. Can I talk to two current clients and one who left?Yes, including someone who left on good terms and can tell you how the hand-back went."Our client list is private." Or only testimonials on their own website.
30. What do your bad reviews say, and what did you change?They know what their complaints say and tell you what they fixed."Those are all fake." Read their BBB complaints and Trustpilot 1-stars yourself before the meeting.

What to ask the references: "Did you get the number of calls they said you would? How fast do they answer you? If you left tomorrow, would you get everything back?"

11

Red flags that end the meeting

Any one of these on the right, and you can thank them and leave. Anything on the left is what you're looking for.

✓Keep talking

  • "Take the contract home and read it."
  • "The domain, profile and accounts are in your name."
  • "Here's the cancellation clause, it's one email."
  • "You'll see Google's charges separately from ours."
  • "Here are three owners you can call."
  • "Here's what depends on you, like answering the phone."

✕End the meeting

  • "Page one guaranteed" or "#1 on Google"
  • The price is only good if you sign today
  • They'll own your domain, Google profile or phone number
  • They won't show you the ad spend separately
  • They offer reviews from "their network", or to filter out unhappy customers
  • They claim to be "from Google" or a "Google partner" that can get you special treatment on Maps
Nobody can pay Google for a better Maps spot

Ads on Maps are labeled as ads. The regular map results can't be bought. Anyone selling "guaranteed Maps rankings" is selling something Google doesn't sell.

13

Quick answers

Is a 12-month contract always bad?

Not always. A longer term can make sense when there's a big setup job, like building a website. What matters is a clear end date, no automatic renewal for another full term, a written way to cancel, and that you keep your website, domain, Google profile and phone number if you leave.

Who should own my domain?

You. The domain should be registered in your business's name, on a registrar account you can log in to. Your marketing company can manage it, but if their name is on it, you can lose your web address when you leave.

Is a call-tracking number a bad idea?

No, tracking numbers are how you find out which marketing makes your phone ring. The problem is when the agency owns the number. Get it in writing that the number transfers to you if you leave, or that it's in your name from day one.

How do I know my ad money really goes to Google?

Ask for admin access to the ads account, or have it billed to your own card. Then you can see exactly what Google charged, separate from the agency's fee.

Should I sign on the first meeting?

No. Take the contract home, read the cancellation and renewal parts, and get any promises from the sales call added in writing. A same-day-only price is pressure, not a deal.
