01

What happens after you miss a call

Picture how a new customer finds you. Their water heater is leaking. They search on their phone. Google shows a few businesses, each with a Call button. They tap the first one. If nobody answers, the next one is one tap away.

You don't need a study to know that. But here's what the published numbers say, and where they come from:

42%said they leave a voicemail when a business doesn't answer. 21% said they immediately call another business. (CallRail survey of 1,000 US consumers.)
15%of missed calls ended in a voicemail, across more than 18 million missed calls on one business phone app. (Quo's own call data.)

Read those with care. Both come from companies that sell phone products. One is what people say they do. The other is what callers on one app did. The exact numbers disagree. The direction doesn't: a big share of the people you miss never leave a message, so you never even know what the job was.

Don't trust the scary stat in an ad

You'll see "80% of callers hang up on voicemail" and "85% never call back" in sales pitches, usually with no source. Your own call log beats any of them. That's the next section.

Who loses most: urgent jobs. A burst pipe, no AC in July, a breaker that keeps tripping. Those callers want someone now, and they'll keep dialing until a person picks up. They're also often the best-paying calls you get.

02

The 7-day call audit

Before you buy any fix, find out what you're actually missing. It takes about 5 minutes a night for one week.

Where to find your calls

  • iPhone: Phone app → Recents → Missed. Recents only holds a limited number of calls, so write them down each night.
  • Android: Phone app → Recents or Call history. Tap a number to see each call.
  • Your carrier's online account: many carriers list calls under usage details. Handy as a backup.
  • Business phone app (Google Voice, RingCentral and the like): the call history usually shows missed, answered and voicemail, and can often be exported.

What to count each day

After a week you'll see a pattern: most misses happen in one or two situations. That tells you which fix to buy, and which ones you don't need.

03

The money math

Three numbers, multiplied. No guessing beyond what you put in.

Missed calls a week × the share that would have booked × your average job = what missed calls could be worth each week. Multiply by 52 for a year.

ExampleNumberWhere it comes from
Missed calls a week6Your 7-day audit
Share that would have booked3 out of 10How many calls you answer turn into jobs
Average job$400Last month's invoices ÷ number of jobs
Each week$7206 × 0.3 × $400

Those are example numbers, not a promise. Two honest adjustments:

  • Some missed callers do come back. If your audit shows you won back 2 of 6, use 4 missed calls instead.
  • Missed callers are often your best calls. Emergencies and after-hours calls tend to be bigger jobs. If that's true for you, your average job number is on the low side.

04

Where calls get missed

Most owners think "I miss calls because I'm busy." The audit usually shows something more specific. Here are the usual leaks and the quickest thing to do about each today.

The leakWhy it happensDo this today
On a jobHands full, in an attic, under a house, on a roof.Fix your greeting (Fix 1) and set up a one-tap "on a job, calling you back" reply for declined calls.
DrivingYou shouldn't be holding a phone, and many states ban it.Turn on your phone's driving auto-reply. Answer hands-free only where it's legal and safe.

06

Fix 1: your voicemail greeting (free, 10 minutes)

Most greetings say "leave a message" and nothing else. Give the caller a reason to wait and a time they'll hear back. Copy this:

Greeting script (about 15 seconds)

"Hi, you've reached [Name] at [Business]. I'm on a job and can't pick up. Leave your name, number and what you need, and I'll call you back within [1 hour], usually sooner. If it's easier, text this number. Thanks for calling."

✓Do

  • Say your business name in the first 3 seconds, so they know they got the right place
  • Give a callback time you'll really keep
  • Offer text, if your number can receive texts
  • Record it somewhere quiet, not in the truck

✕Don't

  • Use the phone's robot default ("The number you have dialed…")
  • Promise "right away" if you mean tomorrow
  • Read out your website, hours and services list
  • Let the box fill up

Pros: free, done today, makes you sound like a real business. Cons: it only helps callers who are willing to leave a message, and many aren't.

07

Fix 2: forward to a second person

If someone else can answer (a spouse, an office helper, a crew lead who's between jobs), have the call go to them when you don't pick up.

  1. Use "forward when unanswered", not "forward everything".Your phone rings first. Only the calls you miss move on. Most carriers call it conditional or no-answer forwarding. Ask your carrier or check its support page for how to turn it on.
  2. Set how many rings first.About 4 to 5 rings (roughly 20 seconds) is a fair window before it moves on.
  3. Give the second person a cheat sheet.What you do, where you work, how soon you can come out, and what to write down: name, number, address, what's wrong.
  4. Test it.Call your own number from another phone, let it ring, and make sure it lands where you expect.

Pros: a real person answers, little or no extra cost. Cons: that person has a life too. Evenings, weekends and their own lunch break are the same gaps you had. If they miss it, it goes to their voicemail, not yours.

08

Fix 3: missed-call text-back

When you miss a call, the caller gets a text from your number within a minute: "Sorry we missed you, this is [Business]. What can we help with? We'll call you right back." People who won't leave a voicemail will often reply to a text.

You can do it by hand with a saved reply, or automatically with a business phone app. The free Missed Call Fix-It Guide has copy-and-paste texts for each situation.

Texting has rules. Follow them.

Automated texts need registration. In the US, carriers require business texting from a regular local number to be registered (called "10DLC"). Your phone provider does this with you. Unregistered texts can be blocked or filtered. Only text people who just called you, about their call. Don't slip a promotion or a review ask into it, and don't add them to a marketing list without their written consent. Honor STOP right away. This isn't legal advice; if you're unsure, ask your provider or a lawyer.

Pros: cheap, fast, works while you're on a job, and you know who called even if they don't leave a message. Cons: a text doesn't book the job, you still have to call back. Some callers are on landlines that can't get texts. And the registration step takes some setup.

09

Fix 4: an answering service

A team of live receptionists answers in your business name, takes the details, and texts or emails them to you. Some can book into your calendar.

✓Good

  • A real person answers, which many callers prefer
  • Covers evenings and weekends if you pay for it
  • No staff to hire or train

✕Watch for

  • Usually billed by the minute or by the call. Well-known US services start at a few hundred dollars a month for a small bundle, and busy months cost more
  • They don't know your trade, so pricing and "can you do this?" questions often get "I'll have them call you"
  • Hold times and quality vary, especially at night

Before you sign: call the service yourself, at 7 pm, pretending to be a customer. Ask what happens to a call that comes in at 2 am. Ask how billing works in a heavy week.

10

Fix 5: an AI receptionist

Software that answers your calls with a natural voice, asks what the caller needs, takes their name, number and address, and sends you the details. Good ones can answer basic questions about your services and area.

✓Good

  • Answers every call instantly, any hour, even three at once
  • Usually cheaper per call than a live service
  • Can be taught your services, towns and policies
  • Sends you a written summary, so nothing gets lost

✕Watch for

  • Some callers don't like talking to AI and hang up
  • It can get odd questions wrong, so it needs clear limits ("I'll have the owner call you about that")
  • Quality varies a lot between products. Call each demo yourself
  • One that answers before you get a chance to puts a robot between you and your best customers

11

Speed to call back

Whatever fix you pick, the callback is where the job is won or lost.

A well-known Harvard Business Review study tested how fast more than 2,000 US companies answered web inquiries. Firms that reached out within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to have a real conversation with the lead as firms that waited longer. That study was about web forms, not phone calls. But a phone caller who got your voicemail is usually more urgent, not less.

  1. Check missed calls first.Between jobs, before email, before anything else.
  2. Newest first.The caller from 5 minutes ago is still deciding. The one from this morning may have already booked.
  3. Call from the number they called.A strange number from you looks like spam, and they won't answer.
  4. No answer? Text right after."Hi, it's [Name] from [Business], just tried you back. When's a good time?"
  5. Open with their problem, not your pitch."You called about your water heater. Is it still leaking?" For what to say next, see what to say when they ask "how much?"
Is your callback showing as "Spam Likely"?

Ask a few customers what their phone showed when you called. If it's a spam label, register your business numbers free at freecallerregistry.com, which sends them to the companies behind most carrier spam labels. It doesn't promise a fix, but it's free and takes a few minutes.

Paying for Google Local Services Ads? Missed calls there cost you twice. See missed calls on Local Services Ads.

12

Quick answers

Don't customers just leave a voicemail?

Many don't. In a CallRail survey of 1,000 US consumers, only 42% said they leave a voicemail when a business doesn't answer, and 21% said they immediately call another business. Count your own calls for a week to see your real numbers.

Is it legal to auto-text people who called me?

A one-time reply to someone who just called you, about their call, is the common, low-risk use. Automated business texting in the US needs your number registered for business texting (10DLC) through your phone provider, and you must honor STOP. Don't add marketing to it without written consent. Ask a lawyer if you're unsure.

Answering service or AI receptionist?

A live answering service gives callers a person but usually bills by the minute or call and knows little about your trade. An AI receptionist answers every call instantly at a lower cost per call, but some callers dislike talking to AI. The best setups ring you first and only answer when you can't.

How fast should I call back?

As fast as you can, ideally within minutes. A caller who reached voicemail is often already dialing the next business. Call back the newest missed calls first, and text if they don't pick up.
