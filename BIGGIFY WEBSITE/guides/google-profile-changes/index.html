Google Business Profile

Change your Google profile without getting hidden or suspended

Changed your name, hours and services in one afternoon, and the next morning you were gone from Maps? That's not bad luck. Some fields make Google re-check your whole business. Here's which ones, the safe order to change them, and how to get back if it already happened.

11 min read Checked against Google's current rules By Wyatt, Biggify

You'll walk away with

  • The 5 edits that make Google re-check you, and the safe order
  • How to add a new or tracking number without breaking anything
  • A tick-off checklist to run before you hit Save

The 20-second version

Jump to the checklist →
  1. Change one big thing at a time. The big ones: business name, address or service area, phone number, main category, website link.
  2. Wait about a week between big changes. Photos, posts and service descriptions are fine any time.
  3. Make your website, truck, invoices and license match first, then change Google.
  4. Never delete your profile and start over. You lose every review and it looks worse to Google, not better.
  5. Own your profile on an email you control. Your marketing company should be a Manager, never the Owner.

01

Which edits are risky

Google treats a few fields as the "core" of your business. Change one and Google may ask you to verify again. Change several at once and it can look like someone hijacked the profile. That's when listings get hidden or suspended.

What you changeRiskHow to do it safely
Business nameHighOnly to your real name as it appears on your sign, truck, invoices and license. Update those first.
Address, or showing/hiding itHighSame profile, never a new one. Expect to verify again.
Service areasMediumList where you really work (Google's limit is about 2 hours' drive). Don't add 40 towns in one go.
Main phone numberMediumSee phone numbers. Keep the old number as an extra number for a while.
Main (primary) categoryMediumOne change, then wait 2 to 6 weeks before judging it.
Website linkMediumLink straight to your own site. No redirect links, no Facebook page in the website box.
HoursLowReal hours only. See hours.
Photos, posts, services, descriptionLowChange any time. Real job photos beat stock photos.
While a verification is pending

Postcard, video, anything: don't touch the name, address or category, and don't ask for a second postcard. A new code cancels the first one.

02

Your business name

Your name on Google has to be your real-world name: what's on your sign, your truck and your paperwork.

✓Allowed

  • Your name exactly as it's on the truck and invoices
  • "Plumbing" or your town in the name, if it's your registered DBA and it's on the truck, site and invoices
  • Renaming the existing profile when you rebrand, so the reviews stay

✕Gets edited back or suspended

  • Stuffing a city or service in: "Mike's Plumbing Port Charlotte Water Heater Repair"
  • Slogans, phone numbers, "Best", "#1"
  • ALL CAPS, unless your logo really is

Why your competitor gets away with it (for now): keywords in the name still help rankings, which is why it's tempting. It's also exactly what gets reported, edited back, and sometimes suspended.

Rebranding? Have the new signage or truck lettering ready first, because Google may ask for a video showing it.

03

Phone numbers and tracking lines

Getting a new business line, a call-tracking number, or an answering service with its own number? This is the order that keeps your profile safe:

  1. New number goes in as the main (primary) phone.That's the one customers tap.
  2. Old or real number goes in as an "additional phone".Google matches your phone against your website and directories, and this keeps the match.
  3. Update your website and Facebook the same week.So everything Google checks agrees.
  4. Keep the old number ringing for a few months.Old customers and old directory listings will keep using it.
W
How we do it for our clientsWyatt, Biggify

When we move a client to a new business line, the phone is the only big field we touch that week. New line as primary, old number as additional, website updated the same day. Hours, categories and anything else wait until the next week.

Whose number is it?

If a marketing company gives you a number they own and you leave them, that number goes with them. Owners have watched their old Google number start ringing another business. Make sure any number on your profile is in your name, or the contract says it transfers to you.

04

Hours and "24/7"

OpenBeing open when someone searches is a confirmed Google ranking factor.
Fake 24/7A known trigger for suspensions, and it burns the 2 am caller who gets voicemail.
  • A business showing "Closed" at 7 pm gets shown far less at 7 pm. That's why longer real hours win calls.
  • Only list hours you really answer.
  • The honest way to show more hours: have someone (a person or an answering service) actually pick up and take the job after hours. Then 24/7 is true.
  • Holidays: set "special hours" a week ahead. If you're open on a holiday, say so. That's when competitors are closed.
05

Address, service area, moving

Work from home and drive to customers?

  • Use your real home address and hide it. Then set your service areas.
  • Never use a PO box, UPS Store box or virtual office. Those get listings removed, and competitors know how to report them.
  • Adding more service areas doesn't make you rank in those towns. Ranking still comes from where your business is. To win nearby towns, give each town a page on your website and earn reviews from customers there.

Moving?

  • Change the address on the same profile. Your reviews move with you.
  • Expect to verify again at the new place, so have the sign up or the truck parked there.
  • If someone already closed the old profile and opened a new one, contact Google support and ask them to move the reviews.
One profile per business

Don't make separate profiles for "drain cleaning" and "water heaters". Use categories and the Services list instead. A second profile is only allowed for a real second location with its own staff, signage and crew.

Matching your info everywhere (what people call "NAP")

NAP means Name, Address, Phone. It's a smaller ranking factor than it used to be, but mismatches still cause trouble: Google sees a different phone or old hours on another site and edits your profile to match. Google's AI answers pull from these sites too.

07

Who should own your profile

This is the one that costs owners the most when it goes wrong.

RoleWhoWhat they can do
Primary OwnerYou, on an email you control. Ideally a business email, not your personal one, so it stays with the business.Everything, including adding and removing people.
ManagerYour marketing company, office manager, web person.The day-to-day work. They can't remove you.

Check who has access every few months: in your profile, open the menu → Business Profile settings → People and access. Remove anyone you don't know or don't work with anymore.

Why it matters: if a manager's Google account gets suspended for something unrelated, the profiles it manages can get pulled down with it. An old employee or agency with access can also change your phone number or delete things.

08

Getting your profile back from an old agency or ex-employee

  1. Ask first, in writing.Ask them to make you Primary Owner, then remove themselves. Only the Primary Owner can hand that role over.
  2. If they won't, or don't answer:go to business.google.com/add, search your business and choose Request access. The current owner gets a few days to respond. If they don't, Google's email may let you claim it by verifying.
  3. If your address is hidden(you're a service-area business), the request-access route often isn't offered. Contact Google Business Profile support and ask to "transfer ownership".
  4. Have proof ready.Business license, registration, a utility bill in the business name, and an email at your own website's domain if you have one.
The 7-day wait is normal

Once you're the owner, Google won't let you remove other users for the first 7 days. Wait it out, then clean up.

09

When strangers edit your profile

Anyone on Google Maps can suggest an edit to your hours, your phone, even "Permanently closed". Google usually checks the edit first, and owners now get an email before big edits go live. Still:

  • Look at your own profile once a week on your phone, logged out. It takes 30 seconds.
  • If something's wrong, fix it from your dashboard. If "closed" keeps coming back, check that your website, Facebook and Yelp don't say closed or show old hours.
  • Check your website and booking links once a month too. Booking links have been hijacked to point at competitors.
10

"This is Google, your listing will be removed" (it isn't Google)

Hang up

Google doesn't cold-call to threaten removal, and the profile is free. Those robocalls and "verification" calls are scams selling you something. Don't press any keys, then log in to your own dashboard to see if there's a real alert. The FTC has warned about these calls by name.

11

If you're already suspended or hidden

  1. Don't make a new profile.It's the most common mistake, and it makes the next suspension more likely.
  2. Find what's off.Check against the sections above: a keyword-stuffed name, a mailbox address, fake hours, a redirect link, a duplicate profile.
  3. Fix it first, then gather proof.License, registration, a utility bill with the exact name and address, photos of the sign or lettered truck.
  4. Appeal through Google's appeal tool.From your Business Profile, or Google's "Business Profile appeals" page. You get one standard appeal per decision. After that, only new evidence gets a second look.
  5. Wait, and don't stack more edits on top.Answers usually take about a week, sometimes 5 to 6.
Don't pay to get "unsuspended"

Owners report paying for that service and still being suspended months later. Everything those services do is in the five steps above, and the appeal itself is free.

Quick answers

Will changing my hours get me suspended?

Hours are a low-risk edit on their own. The risk comes from listing hours you don't really answer, like a fake 24/7, or from changing hours together with your name, address or phone in the same week.

Should I make a new profile if mine gets suspended?

No. A new profile loses every review and makes the next suspension more likely. Fix what's wrong on the existing profile and appeal through Google's appeal tool.

Should my marketing company own my profile?

No. You should be the Primary Owner on an email you control. Your marketing company should be a Manager: they can do the work but can't remove you.

Is a call-tracking number safe on my profile?

Yes, if the tracking number is the main phone, your real number stays as an additional phone, and the number is in your name or transfers to you if you leave.
