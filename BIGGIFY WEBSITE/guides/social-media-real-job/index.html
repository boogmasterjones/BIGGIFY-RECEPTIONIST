Social media

What social media is really for (if you're a trade business)

You're not trying to go viral. Nobody hires a plumber off a dance video. Social media's real job for a trade business is quieter: when someone finds you and checks you out before they call, your page proves you're real, local and busy. Here's how to post for that person, in about 10 minutes a week.

Jump to the 10-minute routine →

01

The real job of your page

Think about how people actually hire a roofer or an electrician. They search Google, ask a neighbor, or see your truck. Then, before they call, a lot of them do one more thing: they look you up to make sure you're legit.

That's the moment your Facebook or Instagram page earns its keep. In about ten seconds of scrolling, they're answering three questions:

Real?A real crew, real trucks, real jobs. Not a lead-selling middleman.
Active?Recent posts. A page that went quiet two years ago looks closed.
Good?Clean work, happy customers, and answers to people's comments.

So stop measuring your page by likes and reach. Measure it by one thing: if a stranger checked it today, would they feel safe calling you?

Do this today

Open your Facebook page on your phone, logged out, like a stranger would. Look at the date of your last post, your phone number and your hours. If any of it is old or wrong, that's your first job.

02

What it does with Google (honestly)

You'll hear "post on Facebook to rank higher on Google." Google doesn't say that. Google says local results come mainly from relevance, distance and prominence (how well-known you are, including your reviews and the websites that link to you). Facebook posts aren't on that list. Here's what social profiles really do for you around Google:

What's trueWhy it matters to you
Your social profiles show up when people search your business name.Your name search is a page of proof. A live Facebook page near the top helps. A dead one hurts.
Google says it pulls business info from several sources, including the web and other users, and can update your profile from them. AI answers pull from across the web too.If Facebook still shows your old phone or hours, it can confuse Google and the AI answers. Make them match. More in matching your info everywhere.
Active, real profiles back up that you're a real, operating business.They're one more piece of evidence, to customers and to anyone checking, that the business on Google is real and working.
You can add your Facebook and Instagram links to your Google Business Profile, in the "Social profiles" section (where Google offers it).One tap from your Google listing to your recent work.
Google Business Profile posts and photos are the "social" that lives on Google itself.Posts show on your profile in Search and Maps. That's where most trade customers find you, so post there too.
If someone promises rankings from social

Anyone selling "guaranteed rankings" from Facebook posts is selling something Google itself doesn't claim. Ask them to show you where Google says it.

One more Google detail: posts older than six months get archived on your Google profile. A steady trickle keeps your "From the owner" section looking alive.

03

How often to post

One to three times a week. That's it. Here's why that number works for a trade business:

  • It's enough to look active. Whenever someone checks, there's something from the last few days.
  • It's little enough to keep real. You do more than three jobs a week. Every post can be a real job, so you never need filler.
  • It's little enough to keep going. A page that posts twice a week for a year beats one that posted daily for three weeks and stopped.

If you can only do one platform, keep your Google profile and Facebook going. Use the same photo for both (see section 09).

04

Photos: real or nothing

Your phone camera is the best marketing tool you own. A plain photo of a real job beats any stock photo, and it beats every AI image.

✓Post this

  • The finished job, straight on, in good light
  • Before and after, shot from the same spot and the same angle
  • Your real crew at work, in uniform, with the truck
  • The problem you found (the corroded pipe, the cracked tile), explained in one line
  • A short video walking through a finished job

✕Skip this

  • AI-generated photos of "your" work or "your" team
  • Stock photos of smiling models in hard hats
  • Blurry, dark or badly cropped shots
  • Other companies' photos, even "for ideas"
  • Generic quote graphics and "Happy Monday!" filler

Why no AI images: people are getting good at spotting them, and a photo that looks fake makes them wonder what else is fake. Your whole page exists to prove you're real. One fake photo undoes that.

Before and afters, done properly

  1. Take the "before" before you touch anything.Once the tools come out, it's too late.
  2. Remember where you stood.Same spot, same angle, same height for the "after". Use a doorframe or a corner as your marker.
  3. Hold the phone level and wipe the lens.A greasy lens is why so many job photos look foggy.
  4. Keep the subject in the middle.Facebook, Instagram and Google crop photos differently. Leave room around the edges so nothing important gets cut off.
  5. Check it before it goes out.Sharp? Level? Nothing private in the frame?

05

Ask before you show their home

It's their house. A quick ask builds trust, and most people say yes, especially when the job looks great.

  • Ask at the end of the job: "This came out great. Mind if I post a photo of it? No address or names."
  • Leave out anything that says where they live: house numbers, street signs, license plates, mail on the counter.
  • Leave out the personal stuff: family photos on the wall, papers, screens, anything in the background you'd mind someone seeing in your own home.
  • Kids' faces are a no, unless the family says yes. If a child is in the shot, crop them out or skip the photo.
  • Customers in the photo? Only if they say yes. Same for tagging them.
Make it a habit

Add "OK to post photos?" to your job sheet or invoice. Tick yes or no. Then nobody on the crew has to remember to ask.

06

Captions in your own voice

Write the way you talk to a customer on the driveway. Short. Plain. What you did, where, and why it matters to them. No hashtag walls, no "Elevate your home!"

Copy these and fill in the blanks:

  1. The finished job"Wrapped up a [job] in [town] today. [One detail about what was wrong or what we did]. Happy with how this one came out."
  2. The problem you found"This is what we found behind [the wall / under the sink / in the attic]. If you're seeing [warning sign], give us a call before it gets worse."
  3. Before and after"Before and after on a [job] in [town]. [How long it took or what changed]."
  4. The crew"[Name] on a [job] this morning. [Years with us, or one true thing about them]."
  5. A quick tip"Quick one: [simple tip a homeowner can do themselves]. Saves a service call."

Only write what's true. Don't name the customer, don't promise prices you haven't quoted, and don't claim anything you can't back up.

07

Reply fast

A comment or message on your page is often a job asking to happen. The person who's waiting for your reply is also messaging the next company.

  • Turn on notifications for page messages and comments in the Meta Business Suite app on your phone.
  • Answer within the hour during work hours when you can. Even "Thanks! Can I call you at this number?" keeps the job warm.
  • Move it to a phone call fast. Price and scheduling go better by voice.
  • Answer every comment, even just "Thank you!" Other people read them.
  • Unhappy comment? Reply calmly, once, in public, then take it to the phone. Never argue in the comments.
Saved reply worth setting up

"Thanks for reaching out to [Business]! What's going on, and what town are you in? If it's easier, call or text us at [number]." Set it as a saved reply so you can send it in two taps from a ladder.

08

What to skip

  • Don't buy followers or likes. They don't call you. They make your real posts look ignored (10,000 followers, 2 likes). And fake engagement is against Meta's rules.
  • Don't chase viral. A video with a million views from people three states away does nothing for your schedule. Fifty views from people in your town can.
  • Don't post to post. No filler graphics, no copied memes, no daily "motivation". If you have no real job to show, skip the day.
  • Don't open accounts you won't keep up. A dead TikTok with three videos from long ago does more harm than no TikTok.
  • Don't argue politics or with customers on the business page. Ever.
09

When you're slammed

Busy season is exactly when people are checking you out, and exactly when posting falls off. Here's the minimum that still works.

One photo from today's job. One line of caption. That's a post. It takes two minutes in the truck before you pull away.

Then use it three times:

WhereHow
Google Business ProfileAdd it as an "Update" post, plus add the photo to your profile photos. This is where most trade customers find you.
FacebookSame photo, same caption.
InstagramSame photo. Meta Business Suite can post to Facebook and Instagram in one go.

One photo, three places, five minutes. Nobody checking you out will mind seeing the same job on Google and Facebook. They'll see that you're working.

Ask your crew

Make "one good photo per job, texted to me" part of the job. By Friday you'll have more than you can post.

10

The 10-minute weekly routine

Pick a time you'll actually keep, like Monday morning coffee or Friday after the last job. Set a phone reminder.

  1. Pick 2 or 3 photos from this week's jobs (2 minutes).The sharpest, best-lit ones. Check for house numbers, faces and anything private.
  2. Write one line for each (3 minutes).Use the caption starters in section 06.
  3. Post or schedule them (3 minutes).Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram. Your Google Business Profile for the Google post. Spread them across the week.
  4. Clear your inbox (1 minute).Any unanswered message or comment from the week gets a reply now.
  5. Glance at your info (1 minute).Phone, hours and website link on Facebook still match Google? Good. Done.

Quick answers

Do Facebook posts help me rank on Google?

Google doesn't say they do. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. What social profiles do is show up when people search your name, back up that you're a real, operating business, and repeat your name, phone and hours across the web.

How often should I post?

One to three times a week is plenty, as long as every post is a real job and real people. Steady and real beats daily and filler. The goal is that anyone who checks your page sees recent work.

Can I use AI-generated photos?

Don't. People are getting good at spotting them, and a fake-looking photo makes them wonder what else is fake. Use real photos from real jobs, even plain phone photos.

Should I buy followers to look bigger?

No. Bought followers don't call you, they make your posts look ignored, and fake engagement is against Meta's rules. A small page with recent real work does the job.
