Marketing a trade business is simpler than they make it sound

Every week someone calls you selling SEO, ads, directories, "AI marketing" or a new app. It sounds like you need all of it. You don't. A trade business needs five things done well and a phone that gets answered. Here's the whole formula on one page, what good looks like, and a 5-minute check for each piece.

15 min read Checked against current rules By Wyatt, Biggify

The 20-second version

Jump to the order to do it in →
  1. The formula: a well-built website + a worked Google profile + active socials + steady reviews. Add a true angle only if your market is crowded.
  2. Then answer every call. All the marketing in the world turns into money only when somebody picks up.

01

The formula on one page

Strip away the jargon and local marketing for a trade business comes down to this. Each piece has one job.

WebsiteProves you're real and turns a curious visitor into a call. It's the one piece you fully own.
GoogleYour profile on Maps is where most local customers find you. It's your shop window.
SocialsShow you're open, busy and doing real work right now. Customers check before they call.
ReviewsHelp you rank and help you win the click. Fresh ones matter as much as the total.
AngleOnly if your market is crowded: one true reason to pick you over the shop with 600 reviews.
PhoneThe piece that turns all of the above into money. An unanswered call wastes every other piece.

How the pieces feed each other

None of these is a separate project. One finished job, handled right, feeds all of them:

  1. You finish a job and take 3 photos.Before, during, after. Real photos of real work, on your phone.
  2. The photos go up on Google and Facebook that week.Your profile looks active, and your page shows real work.
  3. You ask that customer for a review the same day.While they're still happy. Fresh reviews help you show up and help you win the click.
  4. The best jobs become proof on your website.A project write-up or a line on the service page. Your site gets more useful to searchers and to Google.
  5. More people find you, trust you, and call.Your Google profile and website send the calls.
  6. Somebody answers, books the job, and the loop starts again.Miss the call and the loop stops right here.
Why it's simple, not easy

Nothing here is a secret. The hard part is doing it every single week while you're also running jobs. That's where most owners slip, and it's fine to admit it.

02

Piece 1: a well-built website

Your website isn't a brochure. It has two jobs: convince a stranger on a phone that you're the real deal, and make calling you the easiest thing on the page.

What good looks like

  • Your phone number is tappable at the top of every page on a phone, and the page loads fast on mobile data.
  • The first screen says what you do and where: "Water heater installs in [your county]", not "Welcome to our website".
  • A page for each main service and for the main towns you serve, written in plain words, not one page that lists everything.
  • Real photos of your crew, trucks and jobs. Stock photos of smiling models make you look like everybody else.
  • Proof near the top: licensed and insured (if true), reviews, years in business, the towns you cover.
  • You own it: the domain is in your name and you can get the files and logins if you ever leave your web person.

The 3 most common mistakes

✕What we see most

  • Pretty but slow, with the phone buried. Big videos and sliders that take seconds to load on a phone, and the number only in the footer.
  • One "Services" page for everything. Google can't tell what you're best at, and neither can the customer.
  • The agency owns the domain. When you leave, the site and years of work stay with them.

✓The fix

  • Number in the header, tap-to-call, light pages.
  • One page per main service, one per main town you truly serve.
  • Domain, hosting and Google accounts in your name. See who owns your stuff.

03

Piece 2: a worked Google Business Profile

For most trades, this free profile on Google Maps sends more calls than anything else. "Worked" is the key word: set it up once and forget it, and it slowly falls behind the profiles that get attention every week.

Google says local results depend on three things: relevance (does your profile match the search), distance (how close you are) and prominence (how well-known you are, which includes your reviews and your website). You can't move your shop, so you work the other two.

What good looks like

  • The right main category, the most specific one for the work you want most, plus extra categories only for work you really do.
  • Every field filled in: services with short descriptions, service areas, hours you actually answer, a description in plain words, your website link.
  • New real photos every week or two, and a short post with a job photo every week.
  • Every review answered, good and bad, within a few days.
  • You're the Primary Owner on an email you control.

The 3 most common mistakes

MistakeWhy it hurtsThe fix
Main category too broad or wrongYou don't show for the searches you want most.Pick the most specific match for your best-paying work. One change, then wait a few weeks to judge it.
Set it up years ago, never touched sinceOld photos and no posts make you look closed next to an active competitor.A photo and a post every week. 10 minutes.
Changing lots of big things at onceName, address, phone and category changes in one go can trigger a re-check or a suspension.One big change at a time. See changing your profile safely.

04

Piece 3: active socials

For most trades, Facebook and Instagram aren't where the big flood of calls comes from. Their job is proof. People look you up after they find you on Google, and a page with real recent jobs says "open, busy, trustworthy". A dead page from three years ago says the opposite.

What good looks like

  • A post or two a week of real work: before and after, a quick video of the crew, a problem you found and fixed.
  • The same name, phone, website and hours as your Google profile.
  • Captions that sound like you and say the town: "Swapped a 15-year-old tank in [town] today. Here's what the inside looked like."
  • Messages answered the same day. A message is a lead, just like a call.

The 3 most common mistakes

  1. Posting stock graphics and "Happy Monday!" They get ignored and show nothing about your work.
  2. Going quiet for months, then posting ten things in one day.
  3. Ignoring messages. Someone asked for a quote two weeks ago and nobody saw it.

05

Piece 4: steady reviews

Google says review count and review score factor into local ranking. Customers read them too: they compare your count, your stars and how recent your last review was against the next name on the list. "Steady" matters more than "lots at once".

What good looks like

  • You ask every happy customer, every time, the same day the job's done, with a direct link that opens the review box.
  • New reviews arrive every month, not 40 in one week and then nothing for a year.
  • Every review gets a reply written as the business, in first person, that mentions the job when it fits.
  • Bad reviews get a calm, short reply that takes it offline. Never an argument.

The 3 most common mistakes

✕Don't

  • Only asking when you remember. That's how you end up with 23 reviews after 15 years.
  • Paying for reviews, or offering a discount for one. Google bans it, and the FTC's rule on reviews bans rewards tied to a positive review.
  • Only asking the happy ones by filtering unhappy customers away first ("review gating"). Google's rules don't allow it.

✓Do

  • Make asking part of closing every job, like taking payment.
  • Ask everyone. A few honest 4-star reviews make the 5s believable.
  • Text the link. It's easier than asking someone to search for you.

06

Piece 5: answering every call

This is the piece most marketing companies never mention, and for us it's part of the formula. Everything above exists to make the phone ring. If it rings while you're under a house or on a roof and nobody picks up, many callers won't leave a voicemail. They just call the next name on the list.

What good looks like

  • A real answer, day and night, from you, your office, an answering service or a receptionist line, with the details written down.
  • Missed calls get a text back within minutes ("Sorry we missed you, this is [business], what can we help with?").
  • Hours on Google match the hours someone really answers. That's what makes longer hours honest.

The 3 most common mistakes

  1. Not knowing how many calls you miss. Most phones can show you. Few owners have looked.
  2. A full voicemail box, or a greeting that sounds like nobody's coming back.
  3. Calling back tomorrow. By then they've booked someone else.

07

When your market is crowded: find your angle

In a lot of towns the first four pieces are enough. But if you search your trade and see five shops with hundreds of reviews each, doing the same things as them a little better won't get you noticed fast. You need one true reason to pick you. That's your angle.

An angle has to be true, specific and something the big guys don't say. "Quality work, fair prices" is not an angle. Every competitor says it.

Six angles that work for trades

AngleWhat it sounds likeOnly if…
Specialize in one service"Water heaters. That's all we do."That service is a real share of your work and you're happy to turn down the rest, or you put it front and center.
Speed or same-day"Same-day installs, we carry the common sizes on the truck."You can really do it most days. Promise it and miss it, and the reviews will say so.
A niche customer type"Electrical for older homes." "Pools for vacation rentals." "Flooring for landlords between tenants."You already do a fair amount of this work and know its problems.
The problem nobody explains"What your insurance inspection says about your old water heater / roof / panel, and what to do."You understand it well enough to explain it plainly. Stick to what you know is true where you work.
Warranty networksWorking with home warranty companies, or explaining warranty jobs clearly for homeowners.The rates and paperwork work for you. See home warranty jobs.
Local proofJob photos and reviews named by neighborhood. "Done 3 on your street."The jobs are real and the customer is OK with you saying where.

What we did in a crowded marketWyatt, Biggify

For a water heater company we work with, the market was full of big plumbing shops with hundreds of reviews. So instead of competing as another plumber, the angle became the specialist: water heaters only, same-day installs from stock, plus content about older water heaters and home insurance inspections, which the big shops don't talk about. We're still measuring how far it goes.

The angle-finder (15 minutes, pen and paper)

  1. List your last 30 jobs.Write the service, the type of customer and how fast you got there. Your invoices or calendar have it.
  2. Circle the jobs you'd do all day.The best margin, the least hassle, the happiest customers. Look for what they have in common.
  3. Write down the questions customers keep asking."Will insurance cover it?" "How old is too old?" "Can you come today?" Every repeated question is content nobody's writing.
  4. Look at the top 3 competitors' websites and Google profiles.Write what every one of them says. Cross those out. You can't win on them.
  5. Find the overlap:what you're good at + what customers ask + what competitors don't say. That's your shortlist.
  6. Test it against the truth.Can you deliver it next Tuesday, on a busy day? If not, it's not your angle yet.
  7. Fill in the sentence below and put it on your website's first screen, your Google description and your next 4 posts.
Fill in the blanks

"We're the [trade] in [area] for [who / what problem]. Unlike most, we [the true thing the others don't do or say]."

Example shape: "We're the electrician in [town] for older homes. Unlike most, we explain what your insurance inspector will flag before they see it."

08

What to ignore

Half of keeping marketing simple is saying no. These are the ones we see owners spend money on that rarely brings a call.

✕Skip

  • "Guaranteed page one" or "guaranteed #1". Google itself says no one can guarantee a top ranking. Anyone promising it is selling something else.
  • 40 directory listings. A few that match matter. Dozens of obscure ones add little, and each is one more place your info can go out of date.
  • Buying followers or likes. Bots don't call. The FTC's review and testimonial rule also bans knowingly buying fake followers or views for business use.
  • The shiny object of the month. A new app, a new platform, a "secret" trick. If your Google profile is stale and you miss calls, nothing new will fix that first.
  • Fake reviews in any form. Bought, swapped with friends, or written by staff. They get removed, and they can get your profile flagged.

✓Spend that time on

  • Your Google profile, every week.
  • Your name, address and phone matching on your site, Google, Facebook, Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor and Angi.
  • Real job photos and posts.
  • Asking every customer for a review.
  • Answering the phone.
One quick test for any pitch

Ask: "Which of the five pieces does this improve, and how will I see it in calls?" If they can't answer in a sentence, it's a shiny object. Before you sign anything, read the questions to ask before you sign.

09

The order to do it in, starting from zero

If you have nothing yet, or everything is a mess, don't try to fix it all this week. Go in this order. Each step makes the next one pay off more.

  1. Week 1: plug the phone.Count your missed calls, clear the voicemail, set up a text-back, and decide who answers when you can't. No point sending calls to a phone nobody answers.
  2. Week 1 to 2: claim and finish your Google profile.Right category, every field, real hours, 10 real photos. Make sure you're the Primary Owner.
  3. Week 2: start the review habit.Save your review link, text it after every job, reply to every review.
  4. Week 2 to 6: get a simple, fast website.Phone at the top, one page per main service, real photos, in your name. Simple and live beats perfect and unfinished.
  5. From week 3: post real jobs every week.The same photo can go on Google and Facebook. 10 minutes.
  6. Month 2 and on: match your info everywhere and look at your market.If you're still buried under shops with hundreds of reviews, work through the angle-finder.
  7. Every month after: check the numbers.Calls, reviews in the last 30 days, what your Google profile's performance screen shows. Keep what works.

10

Quick answers

What marketing does a trade business actually need?

A well-built website, a complete Google profile you work every week, active social pages showing real jobs, a steady flow of new reviews, and someone answering every call. Only if your market is crowded do you also need a true angle.

Do I need to be on dozens of directories?

No. Make your name, address and phone match on your website, Google, Facebook and the handful you already use, like Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor and Angi. Dozens more add little.

Can anyone guarantee page one on Google?

No. Google itself says no one can guarantee a number one ranking. Treat a guaranteed-rankings promise as a red flag.

Should I buy followers to look bigger?

No. Customers don't hire you for a follower count, bought followers never call, and the FTC's review and testimonial rule bans knowingly buying fake followers or views for business use.

Where do I start if I have nothing?

Answer your phone first, then finish your Google profile, start asking every customer for a review, get a simple fast website, and post real jobs every week. The full order is above.
