Google reviews

Your Google reviews disappeared? The 5-minute check, and what you can actually do

You had 87 reviews yesterday. Today it says 71. Or a customer swears they left you five stars and you can't find it. Owners post about this all the time. Sometimes the reviews are only hidden, sometimes Google's filter pulled them, and sometimes it's Google's own bug. Here's how to tell which in 5 minutes, and what actually helps.

13 min read Checked against Google's current rules By Wyatt, Biggify

  • A 5-minute check: gone, hidden, or a Google glitch
  • The right place to ask for reviews back (it's not the tool most people try)
  • How to collect reviews so the filter leaves them alone

The 20-second version

Jump to the 5-minute check →
  1. Check logged out first. Open a private window and look at your profile. The count can lag, and Search and Maps don't always agree.
  2. Lots gone at once? Look for a Google glitch or a notice on your profile before you change anything.
  3. A few gone? It's usually Google's spam filter. Bursts, new accounts, and reviews from your own Wi-Fi get caught most.
  4. To ask for them back, contact Google Business Profile support. The customer can also appeal from their own Google Maps profile.
  5. Don't buy replacements, re-ask the same people, or start a new profile. Ask every happy customer, steadily, with a direct link.

01

The 5-minute check: gone, or just hidden?

Before you panic or call anyone, work down these steps. Stop at the first one that explains it.

  1. Look from the outside.Open a private (incognito) window, logged out, and search your business name. What you see logged in as the owner isn't always what customers see.
  2. Check Maps and Search both.Open your profile in the Google Maps app and in a Google search. The review count can lag behind for a day or two, and the two don't always match.
  3. Check from somewhere else.Ask a friend in another town to look on their phone, or look on mobile data instead of your shop Wi-Fi. If they see the reviews, it's a display problem, not a removal.
  4. Ask the customer what they see.A filtered review usually still shows to the person who wrote it, on their own account. If they see it and you don't (logged out), Google's filter is holding it.
  5. Read your dashboard and email.Look for a notice about a suspension, a re-verification, paused reviews, or "fake reviews removed". If there's one, jump to profile problems.
  6. Count how many and when.A handful over weeks points at the spam filter. Dozens or hundreds overnight points at a Google glitch or a profile-level event.
Screenshot your count today

Most owners can't say how many reviews they had last week, so they can't tell Google what's missing. Take a screenshot of your review count and newest reviews once a week. If something drops, you have proof.

03

Why real reviews get filtered

Google's spam filter is a machine. It doesn't know your customer is real. It looks at patterns, and some honest habits look exactly like the patterns fake reviews leave behind. Google's own help pages admit its automated system sometimes removes real reviews by mistake.

Google doesn't publish how the filter works. These are the patterns Google's rules name, plus what owners and local search pros see over and over:

  • Bursts. Zero reviews for months, then 25 in a week after a big review push. Google's rules name "unusual volumes or patterns" of reviews as a red flag.
  • Same Wi-Fi or same device. Customers handed your tablet, or asked to review while standing in your office on your Wi-Fi. Lots of reviews from one connection look like one person with many accounts.
  • Brand-new accounts. A Google account with no photo, no other reviews and no history is easier for the filter to doubt.
  • Links, phone numbers or emails in the review. Those look like ads, and get pulled.
  • Incentives. "10% off if you leave a review" or a gift card drawing. Even if the review is honest, it breaks the rules.
  • Copy-paste wording. If you hand customers a script to paste, every review reads the same, and that's a pattern.
"Have them leave it before you leave the house"

Lots of coaches teach this. Asking on the spot is fine. Handing them your phone or tablet, or having them join your Wi-Fi to do it, is the part that gets reviews filtered. Send them the link and let them use their own phone, on their own connection, whenever they get to it.

04

Reviews removed for breaking the rules

Some reviews come down because of how they were collected, not because of the filter. Google's review rules say:

✓Allowed

  • Asking every customer for an honest review
  • Sending a direct link to your review form
  • Asking in person, by text or by email
  • A customer naming your tech on their own
  • Replying to every review, good and bad

✕Gets reviews removed

  • Conflict of interest: you, your staff, ex-staff, family, or a buddy who never hired you
  • Gating: asking only happy customers, or screening with "How did we do?" first and only sending 4 and 5 stars to Google
  • Incentives: money, discounts, free service, raffle entries
  • Asking for specific content, like "please mention Mike by name"
  • Review quotas for your techs
  • Bought reviews, review swaps, or posts made from many accounts

The staff-name rule

Google's rules now say a business can't have staff collect reviews that include specific content, or a set number of reviews. That's widely read as covering the common "mention your tech's name" ask, and the quotas some shops put on techs. A customer who names your tech on their own is fine. Just don't ask for it, and don't put it in your review text.

Gating includes the software kind

Some review tools ask "How was your visit?" first, then send happy customers to Google and unhappy ones to a private form. That's gating. If your tool does it, turn that setting off.

Bought reviews hurt the whole profile

When Google catches fake reviews it doesn't just delete them. Its rules say it can stop new reviews for a set time, hide your existing reviews for a set time, and show customers a warning that fake reviews were removed. Real reviews disappear along with the fake ones.

05

When the whole profile had a problem

Sometimes the reviews aren't the problem. Something happened to the profile they sit on.

  • Suspension. Your whole profile disappears, reviews and all. Fix the cause and appeal with Google's Business Profile appeals tool. Reviews usually come back when the profile does. If some don't, contact support. Google has acknowledged missing reviews after reinstatement as a known issue.
  • Paused reviews. If Google sees a sudden wave of suspicious reviews, it may remove them, pause new reviews and email you. Google says these pauses usually lift within a few days. This can happen after a fake-review attack too, not only if you did something wrong.
  • Merge or duplicate. If you had two profiles (an old one, or one a customer or Google created), Google may merge them or remove one. Reviews can get stranded on the one that's gone. Ask support to move them.
  • Address change or move. Change the address on the same profile and the reviews move with you. Closing the old profile and starting a new one leaves them behind.
  • Category or name change. These don't delete reviews by themselves, but they can make Google re-check or re-verify your business. While that's pending, your profile and reviews can look odd or be hidden.

06

When it's Google's bug, not you

Google has had several bugs where reviews vanished from lots of businesses at once, then came back for many of them once it was fixed. Some owners did not get every review back.

Signs it's a glitch: lots of reviews gone at once, no email or notice from Google, and other owners saying the same thing that week.

Where to check (2 minutes):

  • Search Engine Roundtable (seroundtable.com). Search "reviews". It covers Google review bugs within a day or two, often with Google's statement.
  • Google Business Profile Help Community (on support.google.com). Look for recent threads about missing reviews. Lots of fresh threads at once points to a bug.
  • Google Business Profile's own channels. Google's support account on X and the alerts in your dashboard sometimes say "we're aware of an issue".
If it's a bug, sit tight

Screenshot your profile, write down the date, and file one support request so you're on record. Then leave the profile alone. Edits, reposts and a burst of new review asks during a bug only add confusion.

07

How to ask Google for them back

Lots of owners go straight to the Reviews Management Tool. That tool is for reporting reviews you think break the rules, like a fake 1-star, and appealing once if Google leaves one up. It isn't for getting your own good reviews back.

Path 1: you contact Google support

  1. Go to Google Business Profile Help and choose Contact us.Sign in with the account that owns or manages the profile, and pick your business.
  2. Describe it as missing reviews.Type "missing reviews" and choose the missing review option when it's offered.
  3. Give them the facts.Your Business Profile ID, how many are missing, roughly when they went, reviewer names if you know them, whether new reviews still show up, and your screenshots.
  4. Keep the case number.If you need to follow up, reply on the same case instead of opening new ones.

Path 2: the customer appeals their own review

Google lets reviewers see the status of their reviews on their own Google Maps profile (tap your picture, then your profile, then reviews). A removed review may show as removed or not posted, and for most removals there's an option to appeal. Each removal can only be appealed once, and editing or deleting the review cancels the appeal. If a loyal customer asks how to help, this is the thing to send them.

Path 3: your profile was restricted

If Google emailed you about a restriction (paused reviews, hidden reviews, or a fake-review warning), use the appeal link in that email or the Business Profile appeals tool. Fix the cause first, and send proof if you have it.

Be ready for "no"

Google doesn't promise to bring filtered reviews back, and often doesn't say why it removed them. Ask once, clearly, with proof. Then put your energy into new reviews.

08

What not to do

✕Makes it worse

  • Re-asking the same customers to repost. A filtered review usually gets filtered again, and a wave of reposts is exactly the burst pattern.
  • Buying "replacement" reviews. This is how a few lost reviews turns into paused reviews and a public warning.
  • Starting a new profile. It starts at zero, and duplicate profiles can get both suspended.
  • Paying someone to "restore" your reviews. They have no special line into Google. The support form and appeals are free.
  • Making big profile edits while you wait. Leave the name, address and category alone.

✓Does help

  • Screenshots and a date
  • One clear support request
  • The customer's own appeal
  • Steady new reviews from real customers
  • Replying to the reviews you do have
09

Collecting reviews that stick

The fix for disappearing reviews is mostly in how you ask. Reviews that come in like normal customer behavior tend to stay up.

SteadyA few every week beats 30 in one weekend. Ask every finished job, not once a year.
Direct linkYour own "Ask for reviews" link from your Google profile, sent by text. Fewer taps, fewer people who give up.
  • Ask every customer. Not just the happy ones. That's what keeps you clear of gating, and the occasional 4-star makes your 5-stars more believable.
  • Send the link by text, the same day. They tap it on their own phone, on their own connection. Don't hand them yours.
  • No rewards of any kind. No discounts, no drawings, no gift cards, not even for "honest" reviews.
  • Don't script it. "Would you mind leaving us a quick review?" is fine. Telling them what to write, or whose name to mention, isn't.
  • No links or phone numbers in reviews. If you reply, leave them out of your reply too.
  • One gentle reminder, then stop. Pestering doesn't help and annoys good customers.
  • Reply to every review in your own voice. It shows customers someone's home, and they see it.
Where to find your direct link

Search your business name on Google while logged in as the owner, open your profile, and look for "Read reviews" or "Ask for reviews". Google gives you a short link and a QR code. Save the link in your phone so you can text it in seconds.

Quick answers

Why can my customer see their review but I can't?

When Google's filter holds a review back, the person who wrote it usually still sees it on their own account. Everyone else doesn't. Check from a private window, logged out. If it's missing there, it was filtered or removed, not just slow.

Can the Reviews Management Tool get my reviews back?

No. That tool is for reporting reviews you think break the rules, and appealing once if Google leaves them up. To ask about good reviews that went missing, contact Google Business Profile support and pick the missing reviews issue. The customer can also appeal from their own Google Maps profile if their review shows as removed.

Should I ask the same customers to post again?

Usually not. If the filter caught it once, a repost often gets caught again, and a wave of reposts looks like the pattern the filter is looking for. Let the customer appeal from their own Google Maps profile instead, and keep collecting new reviews at a steady pace.

Will a new profile bring my reviews back?

No. A new profile starts at zero reviews and a duplicate profile breaks Google's rules, which can get both profiles suspended. Fix things on the profile you have.
