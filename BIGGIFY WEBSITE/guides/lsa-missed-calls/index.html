Local Services Ads

Google's Local Services Ads now charge for missed calls: what to change this week

You used to pay only when you talked to someone. Now a call that rings out to voicemail can be a charged lead too. Here's exactly which calls count, how to see what you paid for, and the four settings to fix before next week's bill.

11 min read Checked against current rules By Wyatt, Biggify

You'll walk away with

  • Exactly which missed calls Google bills, and which it doesn't
  • The hours and schedule setup that stops paying for rings nobody hears
  • A 15-minute checklist to run this week

The 20-second version

Jump to the checklist →
  1. A missed call is now a charged lead if it comes in during your business hours and the caller hangs on for more than 20 seconds.
  2. Voicemail counts as answered. If it picks up during business hours and the call lasts past 20 seconds, you pay, message or not.
  3. Only run your ads when someone will really pick up. Missed calls are only billed inside both your ad schedule and your business hours.
  4. There's no dispute button anymore. Google credits bad leads on its own, and no longer credits "wrong area" or "wrong job" leads. Tighten your services and areas instead.
  5. Full this week? Pause. A paused campaign costs nothing. An unanswered one costs you twice.

01

Which calls you now pay for

Local Services Ads (the ads at the very top of Google with the "Google Verified" check) still charge per lead, not per click. What changed is what counts as a lead. Google's own lead rules now say this:

What happened on the callCharged?The detail that matters
You answered and talked about a jobYesJudged on the conversation, not how long it rang. Same as before.
Missed call, caller stayed on 20+ seconds, during business hoursYesThis is the new one. Business hours come from your Google Business Profile.
Went to voicemail or a recorded greeting, 20+ seconds, during business hoursYesGoogle treats voicemail as a connected call. You pay whether they leave a message or not.
Missed call under 20 secondsNoStill hurts you, though: missed calls can lower your ranking (see below).
Missed call outside your ad schedule or business hoursNoIf you use an ad schedule, a missed call is only billed when it lands inside both the schedule and your hours.
Phone menu ("press 1 for…") and they hang up without pressing anythingNoIf they press a key, the 20-second clock starts from that press.
Same customer calls or texts again within 15 daysNoCharged once, if it's the same phone number or email. After 15 days of silence, a new call can be a new lead.
You missed a short call and they called backOnceThe callback is judged like any answered call. You pay once, on the first call that qualifies.
Spam, robocalls, duplicatesNoGoogle filters these and says it has added new protections against robot calls.
The double hit

A missed LSA call now costs you the lead price and the job, because most callers just call the next ad down. And Google's ranking page says plainly that missed calls can hurt your "responsiveness", which is one of the things that decides who shows first. Miss calls, pay more, show lower.

Not sure how many calls you miss now? Check your phone's call log for one normal week, count the ones nobody called back within 10 minutes, and run that number through the missed-call calculator. More on why those callers don't wait in who gets the call you missed.

02

The other LSA changes you'll notice

Three more things moved around at the same time. None of them need a big decision, but each one has a small to-do.

1. LSA is moving inside Google Ads

  • Your Local Services Ads become a pay-per-lead campaign inside Google Ads (Google calls it Performance Max). It rolls out in waves. Google emails you about 14 days before your account moves.
  • Still pay-per-lead, same spots on Search and Maps. No keywords to manage.
  • The old LSA dashboard goes away, and its performance reports don't come with you. Your past leads and messages do carry over. To-do: download your reports now if you haven't moved yet.
  • Manual bidding ("max per lead") is gone. You get one target cost per lead for the whole campaign. If you run two trades with very different lead prices, ask for separate campaigns.
  • Weekly budget becomes a daily one (your weekly number divided by 7). Monthly spend can reach about 30 times the daily budget, so check the math.
  • BBB callouts are dropped. Add other short callouts instead, like "Same-day service" or "Family owned", if they're true.

2. Google Verified replaced the old badges

"Google Guaranteed", "Google Screened" and "License Verified by Google" are now one green Google Verified check. The money-back program that came with the old badge has ended. If you were already verified, your badge switched over automatically, and it moves into Google Ads with you.

To-do: make sure your license and insurance on file haven't expired. If they lapse, your verification and your ads can be put on hold.

3. LSA reviews now come from your Google Business Profile

There's no separate LSA review link anymore. Your Google profile's reviews show on your ad. Details in section 9.

Changing your name or address?

Your name, address and hours now feed your ads straight from your Google profile. A big name or address change triggers a verification review, and your campaign can pause until it clears. Do it on a slow week. The safe order is in changing your Google profile without getting suspended.

03

How to see which calls you were charged for

Every lead shows one of four labels: Not charged, Charged, In review or Credited.

  1. Find your leads.Still on the old LSA dashboard: menu (top left) → Leads. Already moved to Google Ads: Goals → Conversions → Leads.
  2. Filter to "Charged".Those are the ones on your bill.
  3. Open each charged call and listen.In the US and Canada, calls through Google's forwarding number are recorded (the caller hears a short notice). Ten minutes of listening tells you more than any report.
  4. Mark each one:answered or missed, real job or junk, booked or not. You'll use this in section 8.
Do this every Monday

Same 10 minutes each week: count charged calls, count missed charged calls, count bookings. If missed charged calls aren't close to zero, the fix is in sections 5 to 7, not in your budget.

04

Bad leads: the dispute button is gone, here's what works

Owners who've run LSA for years remember disputing junk leads one by one. That's over. Google now reviews every lead itself, and manual disputes aren't supported in Google Ads.

✓How credits work now

  • Google checks a lead when it first comes in. Obvious junk isn't charged at all.
  • Charged leads get re-checked for about 30 days. If Google decides one was low quality, it credits your balance automatically, usually within 30 days.
  • You can rate any lead: open it and choose Rate this lead. Google says it uses this feedback and sometimes credits leads reported this way.

✕No longer credited

  • "Job type not serviced": someone calls for a service you don't do.
  • "Geo not serviced": someone calls from outside your area.
  • Missed calls that meet the 20-second rule. Those are valid leads now.

So the real fix is upstream. Because wrong-job and wrong-area calls are now yours to pay for:

  • Untick every job type you don't want. If you don't do mobile home plumbing or commercial roofs, turn them off.
  • Cut service areas to where you'll actually drive. Every extra town is more calls you'll turn down and still pay for.
  • Rate every junk lead anyway. It takes 20 seconds and it's the only lever left.
  • Seeing lots of spam that got charged? Collect the examples and call Google Ads support. Keep it factual: dates, numbers, recordings.
05

Hours and ad schedule: only be "live" when someone answers

This is the single biggest fix. Two settings decide when a missed call can be billed, and they live in two places.

Business hoursSet in your Google Business Profile. Flows one way into your ads. Missed calls in these hours can be charged.
Ad scheduleSet in your ads (Campaigns → Settings in Google Ads). If you use one, missed calls are only charged inside both.
  1. Write down the hours someone actually picks up.Not the hours you'd like to answer. If nobody answers before 8 or after 5, those are your hours.
  2. Set your ad schedule to exactly those hours.This is the safe lever: it controls when you're live without touching your Google profile.
  3. Keep your Google profile hours honest, but don't shrink them just for the ads.Being open when someone searches helps you show on Maps. If you list long hours, have real answering coverage for them (section 7).
  4. Lunch and job-site gaps count.If you're under a house from 11 to 1 with no one on the phone, either cover that window or take it out of the schedule.
  5. Holidays:set special hours on your profile ahead of time, and pause or trim the schedule if no one is working.
Don't game it with a phone menu

Yes, callers who hang up at "press 1" without pressing anything aren't charged. But every one of those is a customer you lost, and they still count against you as a missed call. A menu that saves you a lead fee by losing the job isn't a saving.

06

Pausing when you're full

Booked out two weeks? Down a truck? Don't let the ads keep running and the calls roll to voicemail. That's paying for leads you can't take and teaching Google you don't answer.

  • In Google Ads: Campaigns → Settings → pick the LSA campaign → switch the status to Paused.
  • On the old LSA dashboard: look for the pause option on your budget or ad settings page. Can't find it? Set the ad schedule to no hours for now.
  • Pause, don't delete. Your reviews, verification and history stay. Turning back on takes a click.
  • Set a reminder to turn it back on the day your schedule opens up. The most common pause mistake is forgetting.
  • Half-full? Trim the schedule to your best hours or untick the job types you can't fit, instead of pausing everything.

Missed one anyway? Call back fast

08

Track LSA leads to booked jobs

Google tells you what you spent. Only you know what you made. Without this, you can't tell if LSA is your best channel or a leak.

Keep one simple sheet (or use your CRM) with a row per charged lead:

ColumnWhat goes in it
Date and caller numberFrom the lead page.
Charged amountFrom the lead page. Mark it if it's later credited.
Answered or missedMissed and charged is the number you're driving to zero.
Job typeShows which services are worth leaving switched on.
Booked? Job value?Fill in when the invoice is paid.
  • Once a month, do three sums: total spent ÷ jobs booked = cost per booked job. Revenue from LSA jobs ÷ spend = what each ad dollar brought back. Missed-and-charged ÷ all charged = your leak.
  • Mark jobs as booked on your leads page too, where it offers it, so your Google numbers match your sheet.
  • Turn off job types that never book. That's money straight back.

If your leads live in a notebook, a phone log and three text threads, read a CRM for trades that you'll actually use.

09

Reviews inside LSA

Google's ranking page names your rating and your number of reviews as factors in who shows first. And LSA reviews now come from your Google Business Profile, not a separate system.

  • Throw out the old LSA review link. It stopped working. Use your Google profile's review link everywhere: texts, invoices, the back of your card.
  • Ask every happy customer, the same day. A steady trickle looks natural. A sudden pile can get reviews filtered.
  • Reply to every review from your Google profile, good or bad, in your own voice.
  • Don't offer anything for reviews, and don't ask only the happy ones in a way that filters out the rest. Both break Google's rules.
  • Some old LSA-only reviews may not have carried over if they didn't pass Google's normal review rules. Nothing to do there but earn new ones.

The full system, with word-for-word texts, is in reviews that stick, and the free review kit has the templates.

Quick answers

Does LSA charge for missed calls now?

Some of them. A missed call during your business hours is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. With an ad schedule, it has to fall inside both the schedule and your hours.

Am I charged if it goes to voicemail?

Yes, during business hours. Google counts voicemail or a recorded greeting as a connected call and charges it after 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message.

Can I still dispute a lead?

Not with the old dispute button. Google reviews leads itself and credits invalid or low-quality ones, usually within 30 days. Rate bad leads with the feedback survey. Wrong-area and wrong-job-type leads are no longer credited.

If the same customer calls back, am I charged twice?

Not if they use the same number or email within 15 days of your last contact. After 15 days of no contact, a new call can be a new lead.

What happened to Google Guaranteed?

It's now one Google Verified badge, replacing Guaranteed, Screened and License Verified. The money-back program tied to the old badge ended. Already verified? Your badge switched over automatically.
