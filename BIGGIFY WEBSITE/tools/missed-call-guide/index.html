The Missed Call Fix-It Guide

What to do about the calls you can't pick up

A free guide from Wyatt at Biggify · gobiggify.com

You can't answer on a ladder, under a sink or in a crawl space. But a caller who hits a generic voicemail often just calls the next business on the list. This guide gets more of those callers to wait for you.

1. Know your number first (2 minutes)

Before fixing anything, see what the leak is worth. Grab a pen:

  1. How many calls do you miss in a week?           
  2. Out of 10 callers, how many usually book?           
  3. What's an average job worth? $          

Missed calls a week × (booked out of 10 ÷ 10) × average job × 52 = what missed calls could be worth to you in a year.

Or use the free calculator at gobiggify.com, which does the math for you.

2. Fix your voicemail greeting (10 minutes, today)

Most greetings say "leave a message" and nothing else. Give callers a reason to wait and a time they'll hear back.

Copy this (about 15 seconds):

"Hi, you've reached [Name] at [Business]. I'm on a job right now and can't pick up. Leave your name, number and what you need, and I'll call you back within [1 hour / 2 hours], usually sooner. If it's easier, text this number and I'll get right back to you. Thanks for calling!"

Why it works:

  • It says why you didn't answer (you're working, which is a good sign).
  • It promises a callback time.
  • It gives people who hate voicemail a second way to reach you (text).

Only promise a callback time you'll actually keep.

3. Text back every missed caller (copy and paste)

Send these yourself from your business phone to people who called you.

When you see the missed call:

Hi, this is [Name] from [Business]. Sorry I missed your call. I'm on a job. What can I help you with? I'll call you back within the hour.

After you got their voicemail:

Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Got your message about [the job]. I can call you at [time]. Does that work, or is another time better?

If you can't call back today:

Hi [Name], [Your name] from [Business] here. Running late on a job today, but I didn't want you waiting. Can I call you first thing tomorrow around [time]?

4. Set up your phone (5 minutes)

  • Quick replies for declined calls. If you have to decline a call, send a one-tap text instead of silence.
    • iPhone: Phone settings → Respond with Text → set one to "On a job, I'll call you right back".
    • Android: Phone app → Settings → Quick responses.
  • Save your text-backs (section 3) as notes or keyboard shortcuts so each one is 2 taps away.
  • Check your voicemail box isn't full. A full box means callers can't leave anything at all.
  • Driving? Turn on your phone's driving mode auto-reply so callers get a text instead of silence.

5. The 5-minute callback habit

The faster you call back, the more likely you'll still be the one they hire. They may already be dialing the next business.

  • Between jobs, check missed calls first, before email or anything else.
  • Call back the newest ones first. They're most likely still deciding.
  • Write down every missed call for one week: day, time and whether you got the job. That one week shows you where the leaks are, and makes your section 1 number real instead of a guess.
