The first screen

Most of your visitors are on a phone, often standing next to a leak, a dead AC unit or a tripped breaker. What they see before they scroll decides whether they call you or hit Back. That first screen has one job: get the call.

It must answer four questions in five seconds

  1. What do you do?In the words customers use. "Water heater repair and replacement", not "Complete home comfort solutions".
  2. Where?Your main town or county, in the headline or right under it. Someone two states away should know at a glance that you're not for them.
  3. How do I reach you?A big Call button with the number written out, and a second button that jumps to your quote form.
  4. Why you?One or two true facts: licensed, a strong rating if you really have it, how fast you call back. And a real photo of you or your work.

Headline formulas you can copy

FormulaExample
[Service] in [area]Drain cleaning and sewer repair across Greater Tulsa
[The problem], fixed in [area]No AC? Repairs and new systems in Mesa and Gilbert
[Trade] for [town] homes, [true detail]Licensed electricians for Boise homes, from panels to EV chargers

Keep it to three lines on a phone, one idea. If you must choose, clear beats clever. Under it, one plain sentence in your own voice: "We repair and replace water heaters across Mesa, Gilbert and Chandler, and we call back the same day." Only say what's true.

The phone number: big, written out, tappable

  • Write the number as text, never inside a picture. People copy it, and Google reads it.
  • Make it tap-to-call. On any platform, the link looks like this (+1, then 10 digits, no spaces):
<a href="tel:+19415550123">Call (941) 555-0123</a>
  • In the header of every page. On phones, add a Call bar that stays at the bottom of the screen once they scroll past the top.
  • Thumb-sized buttons: at least 44 pixels tall. Call comes first, then "Get a quote".
  • One number everywhere on the site. The full playbook's call tracking chapter covers why.

Where the form goes

Not squeezed into the top of a phone screen, where it pushes your headline and Call button down. This is the order we use on the sites we build:

  1. First screen: headline, Call button, "Get a quote" button, a real photo.
  2. Real job photos, so they see the work is real.
  3. The quote form. The "Get a quote" button jumps straight here.
  4. Reviews, word for word.
  5. Everything else: services, towns, about you, questions.

On service and town pages, put the form high too, not only at the very bottom.

✓Do

  • A real photo of you, your truck or a finished job
  • The number written out and tappable
  • One clear headline with your town in it
  • A still photo that loads fast

✕Don't

  • Rotating sliders (nobody waits for slide 3)
  • Pop-ups or chat bubbles covering the Call button
  • "Welcome to our website"
  • Stock photos of handshakes or models in hard hats
The 5-second test

Open your site on your phone. Hand it to someone who doesn't know your business and take it back after 5 seconds. Ask: what do we do, where, and how would you reach us? If they miss one, fix the first screen before anything else.
