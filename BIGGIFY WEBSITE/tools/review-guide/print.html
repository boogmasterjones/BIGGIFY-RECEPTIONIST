The 30-Second Review Ask

How to get more Google reviews without it feeling weird

Most of your customers are happy. Most of them never leave a review, because nobody asked them at the right moment in an easy way. Here's the whole system on one page.

1. Ask at the "wow" moment, not a week later

The best time is right when they see the finished work: the new floor, the hot water back on, the panel that finally works. Happiness is highest then and fades fast. A week later, you're a stranger asking a favor.

Rule: ask in person before you leave, then send the text the same day.

2. What to say (in person, about 30 seconds)

"I'm really glad it came out the way you wanted. Can I ask a favor? Reviews are how a small business like mine gets found. Would you leave me a quick Google review? I'll text you the link right now so it takes a minute."

Then send the text while you're still standing there. "Later" usually means never.

3. The text (copy and paste)

Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Thanks again for having us out today! If you have a minute, a Google review would mean a lot to a small business like ours: [your review link]. Thank you!

Your review link: search your business on Google, open your profile, tap "Ask for reviews" (or "Get more reviews") and copy the link. Save it as a note or a text shortcut so it's always one tap away.

4. One follow-up, then stop

If no review comes in after 3 days, send one friendly reminder:

Hi [Name], just a quick nudge in case the link got buried: [link]. No worries if you're busy. Thanks again for choosing [Business]!

One follow-up only. More than that annoys people.

5. Reply to every review, within 2 days

Replies show future customers you care, and Google shows them right under the review.

  • 5 stars: thank them by first name and mention the job. "Thanks, Linda! That kitchen backsplash was a fun one. Enjoy it!"
  • Mixed or negative: stay calm, thank them, own what's fair, and take it offline. "Thanks for the feedback, Tom. That's not the experience we want. Please call me directly at [number] so I can make it right." Never argue in public. Never share private details.

Write replies as yourself, in the first person ("I", "we"), not "the company".

6. The rules (these keep your profile safe)

  • Ask everyone, not just the happy ones. Google doesn't allow "selectively" asking for positive reviews. Asking only happy customers ("review gating") can get reviews removed.
  • Never pay or reward for reviews: no discounts, gift cards or entries in a draw. Google forbids it, and US federal rules bar rewards that depend on a positive review.
  • Never write reviews for yourself, or have family or staff do it.
  • Let them use their own phone. Reviews posted from your tablet or phone at the job may be filtered out by Google.

7. The 7-day habit

DayWhat you do
Every jobAsk in person + send the text before you leave
3 days laterOne reminder to anyone who didn't review
Every MondayReply to every new review from last week

That's it. Do this on every job for a month and you'll see the difference on your profile. No one can promise a number of reviews, but asking every customer the right way is the single biggest thing in your control.
