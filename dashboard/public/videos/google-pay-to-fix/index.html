"Google" calls

Someone wants money to fix your Google profile? Here's what I'd do

Say someone calls and says they're Google. They want money to fix your profile. I'd hang up and not press any buttons. My rule of thumb: if "Google" calls or texts me, I assume it's not Google. Google emails me from a real Google address. And Google says it never asks for payment info over the phone. Then I'd log in to the profile myself and look.

01

What I'd do, step by step

Someone calls. They say they're Google. They say your profile has a problem, and they want money to fix it.

  1. Hang up. Don't press any buttons. Don't "press 1 to speak to a specialist".
  2. Don't pay anything. Don't give out card or bank info.
  3. Log in to the profile yourself and look. Is there really a problem?
  4. If it's suspended, use Google's own appeal form. And never make a new profile.
02

My rule of thumb

If "Google" calls or texts me, I assume it's not Google.

Google emails me, from a real Google address. Not a stranger on the phone.

That's my own rule, from what I've seen. Google's help page says real Google calls can happen. But here's what Google itself says about them:

What Google says

Google says it will never ask for payment info over the phone. It won't promise a special spot on Google. Hang up on calls that seem off.

03

If the profile really is suspended

Google's appeal steps:

  • Don't make a new profile.
  • Fix the problem first, then use Google's appeal form.
  • Send proof, like a shop license or a power bill.
  • You only get one try, so get it right the first time.

You don't need a stranger on the phone for any of that.

04

Quick answers

Does Google call businesses?

Google's help page says it can. But it never asks for payment info over the phone. And it never promises a special spot. My own rule? A call or text from "Google" isn't Google. I check my profile myself.

My profile really is suspended. Should I pay someone to fix it?

Use Google's own appeal form. Google says don't make a new profile. Send proof, like a shop license or a power bill. And you only get one try.
