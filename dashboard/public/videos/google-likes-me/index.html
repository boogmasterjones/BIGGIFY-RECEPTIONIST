Google's 3 rules

How I make Google like my trade businesses over their competitors

01

Google tells you how it picks

Google tells you, in writing, how it picks who shows up. It's on Google's own help page about local ranking. Most contractors have never read it.

It looks at three things. In plain words:

  1. Relevance: do you do what they searched for?
  2. Distance: are you close to them, or to the town they typed?
  3. Prominence: do people know you? Google looks at your reviews, and what the rest of the web says about you.

Here's what I do about each one.

03

Distance: don't cheat it

Distance is the one you can't really change. So I don't try to cheat it.

  • I set real service areas. Only towns they drive to.
  • I only make town pages for towns they really serve.
  • I never use a fake address. Ever. That can get a profile suspended.

More on that on the fake address page.

04

Prominence: get known

  1. Reviews first. Steady reviews, every week, and a reply to each one. There's a whole page on how I get them.
  2. A Google post every week.
  3. Real photos from real jobs. Never AI pictures.
  4. The same name, phone and address everywhere. When it all matches, nothing gets mixed up.

None of this is a quick trick. It builds slowly at first. Then it stacks up, week after week.

05

How I know it's working

Here's the thing most owners check that I don't: where they show on the map.

That spot changes with where you stand. Move a mile, and you can get a new list. So it can't tell you much.

I track calls and website clicks from Google. Say they go from 20 a month to 30. That's the number I watch. You can see yours on your own Google profile, in the part that shows calls and clicks.

No promises

Nobody can promise you a spot on Google. These are the things Google says it looks at, and what I do about each.

Quick answers

How does Google decide which local businesses to show?

Google's own help page names three things: relevance (how well you match the search), distance (how far you are from the search location) and prominence (how well known you are, including your reviews and what the web says about you).

Can I pay Google to rank higher on Maps?

Not in the regular map results. Ads are labeled as ads. The rest has to be earned.

Should I check where I show up on the map?

I don't track it. The list changes with where you're standing. I track calls and website clicks from Google instead.
