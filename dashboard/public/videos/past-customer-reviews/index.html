01

Every past job is a review you never asked for

Most owners only ask for reviews on new jobs. That's slow. You get one or two a week.

But think about every job you did last year. And the year before. Every one of those is a review you never asked for.

So my clients go back to past jobs.

02

How the pace works

Here's an example. Say you have 300 past jobs.

  1. Week one: text a few dozen. Start with the newest jobs. They remember you best.
  2. Every week after: a small batch. Keep going down the list.
  3. Anyone who texts STOP never gets another text.

Never the whole list in one day. Google's rules name "unusual volumes or patterns" of reviews as a warning sign. Nothing for months, then 30 in a weekend, is exactly that.

Steady beats a burst

A few new reviews each week look real and busy. That's also what helps you most. Here's what I've seen. After about 30 reviews, what counts is how many new ones come in each week.

03

The text

One kind text each, from the business number. Something like this:

For a past customer

"Hi Sarah, it's Sam's Plumbing. We did your water heater a while back. If it's held up, an honest Google review would really help us."

Then the review link. (Sarah, Sam's Plumbing and the water heater are just examples. Use your own.)

04

Rules I keep

  • Only people who gave you their number for the job. No bought lists.
  • One ask, then up to two reminders, a week apart. Then stop.
  • Ask everyone, not just the ones you think liked you.
  • No gifts or discounts for a review.
05

Quick answers

Can I text my whole customer list at once?

I don't. Google's rules name unusual volumes or patterns of reviews as a warning sign. A small batch each week looks like a real, busy business.

Who should I text first?

The newest jobs. They remember you best.
