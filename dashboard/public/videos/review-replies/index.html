01

Who the reply is really for

A bad review hurts. A bad reply can hurt more.

Here's the thing to remember. The reply isn't for the angry person. It's for every new customer who reads it later. They're asking one thing: "If something goes wrong, how will this shop treat me?"

02

How my replies are set up

  1. Written as the business, in first person. "Thanks for having us out," not "The company appreciates your feedback."
  2. Four and five stars get a warm thank-you. By name, about the job they had done.
  3. Anything under four stars comes to me first. Nothing goes out until I say OK.

That last rule is the big one. A one-star review at 9 at night is when people write replies they regret.

03

What's never allowed in a reply

✓In a reply

  • Thank them by name
  • Talk about the job
  • Sound like a person
  • Ask them to call so you can talk it through

✕Never in a reply

  • Promises
  • Refund talk
  • Blame
  • Legal words

Those four can turn one bad review into a fight everyone can read. Talk about those things on the phone, not in public.

04

An honest note

My clients do good work. I've never had to reply to a one-star for them yet. But it's set up for the day one comes, so nobody has to think about it at 9 at night.

Good vs bad

Bad: "Thank you for your feedback. We value all our customers." That sounds like a robot. Good: "Thanks, Sarah! Glad the new water heater is working. Call us any time." That sounds like the shop.

05

Quick answers

Should I reply to every review?

Yes. I reply to every review, as the business. It shows people reading later that a real person cares.

What do I say to a 1-star review?

Keep it calm and short, written as the business. No promises, no refund talk, no blame and no legal words in public. Ask them to call so you can talk it through. And don't send it while you're angry.
