How I get my trade businesses the most Google reviews possible

Why reviews come first for me

To me, reviews are the most important part of your marketing. People read them before they call. And Google looks at them too.

From what I've seen, around 30 reviews is when the phone starts ringing. After that, what matters is how many new ones you get each week.

Here's an example of why it pays. Say ten new reviews bring you four more calls. Say two of those turn into jobs. That's more money from the same good work.

02

Step 1: Ask right after the job

Ask while they still love the work. Not next week. Today, before you're down the street.

Send it from the business number, so they know who it is. Here's my text. Swap in your names:

The first ask

"Hi Sarah, this is Sam's Plumbing, texting from our new business line. Thanks for having us out today! Would you leave us a quick Google review?"

Then the link, and that's it. It's short, it's kind, and they know who sent it. (Sarah and Sam's Plumbing are just example names.)

The big one: a QR code card

Hand them a QR code card before you leave. It gets a lot more people to leave a review.

Why does it work so well? A text gets buried under other texts. A card sits on the counter, right there when they have a minute.

03

Step 2: Make it one tap

The link should open the review box. Not a Google search. Not your profile, where they have to hunt for the button.

My free kit does this for you. You put in your business name and your review link. It makes your QR card and 20 ready texts.

04

Step 3: Follow up twice, then stop

If they don't leave one, I follow up twice. Each one is a week apart.

Text 2, a week later

"Hi Sarah, it's Sam's Plumbing again. If you have a minute, a quick review would really help us."

Text 3, the last one

"Last one from us, Sarah. No worries if not. Here's the link."

Then I stop. Phone carriers want you to stop, and nobody likes a pest. If someone texts STOP, we never text them again.

05

Step 4: Go back to past customers

Every job you did before is a review you never asked for.

But don't text them all in one day. Start with a few dozen, then a small batch each week. My free guide shows who to text first, how many a week and what to say.

06

Step 5: Reply to every review

Reply to every review, as the business. It should sound like a person, not a robot.

A bad reply sounds like a stranger wrote it: "Thank you for your feedback. We value your business." A good one talks to them, like a friend: "Thanks, Sarah! Glad the new water heater is working. Call us any time."

07

What I never do

✓What I do

  • Ask every customer, on every job
  • Send it from the business number
  • Stop after the third text

✕What I never do

  • Pay for reviews
  • Give a gift or discount for one
  • Only ask the ones who seem happy
08

Quick answers

How many times should I ask for a review?

I ask once right after the job, then follow up twice, about a week apart. Then I stop. If someone texts STOP, they never get another text.

Should I only ask happy customers?

No. I ask every customer, on every job. Picking only the happy ones isn't fair to people reading the reviews, and it's against Google's rules.

Can I give a discount for a review?

I never pay for reviews and I never give a gift for one.

How many reviews do I need?

From what I've seen with my own clients, around 30 is when the phone starts to ring. After that it's about how many new ones come in each week. That's my experience, not a Google rule.
