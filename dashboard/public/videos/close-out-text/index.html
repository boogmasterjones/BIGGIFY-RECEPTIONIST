The close-out text

The 5-word text that brings back ghosted estimates

When an estimate goes quiet after you've sent it and checked in, wait about a week and send five words: "Should I close this out?" It works because it's easy to answer and nobody likes to lose an option. A lot of the time they write back, "No, wait. Let's book it." If they picked someone else, they tell you, and you stop.

01

The text

The close-out text

"Should I close this out?"

That's the whole text. Five words. It's one of the texts I give my trade businesses.

02

When to send it

Say you sent the estimate. You checked in. Still nothing.

So about a week later, you send this. Not on day one. It's the last step, after the others.

03

Why it works

  1. It's easy to answer. Yes, no or not yet. No long reply needed.
  2. Nobody likes to lose an option. "Close it out" makes them decide if they want to keep it.

So a lot of the time, they write back, "No, wait. Let's book it."

04

When they write back

  • "Let's book it": give them two days to pick from. Make yes easy.
  • "We went with someone else": thank them and stop. Now you know, and you can stop wondering.
  • "Not yet": ask when you should check back. Write that date down.
Then stop

If they picked someone else, they tell you. Then you stop. No more texts.

05

Quick answers

What do I text when a customer ghosts my estimate?

About a week after your last check-in, send: "Should I close this out?" It's easy to answer, and nobody likes losing an option.

Isn't that rude?

No. It's polite and short. It gives them an easy way to say yes, no, or not yet.

Know an owner this would help?

Send it to them.
