Estimates

How I turn more estimates into booked jobs

Most estimates that go quiet aren't a no. People just get busy. So I give my trade businesses five steps. Ask two questions before leaving. "When are you hoping to get this done?" 01

Then nothing

You drove out, measured, and wrote the estimate. Then nothing. No call. No text.

Most of those aren't a no. People just get busy. Here are the texts I give my trade businesses to win them back.

02

Why it's worth it

Here's an example. Say you give 10 estimates a month, and 3 turn into jobs.

Now say a few texts win back just 2 more. That's 5 jobs, not 3. Same driving. More money.

03

Step 1: two questions before you leave

It starts before you leave their house. There are two questions I tell my trade businesses to ask.

Question 1

"When are you hoping to get this done?"

Now you have a date to follow up on. And they gave it to you.

Question 2

"Who else needs to see this?"

A wife, a husband, a landlord. That's often why a job gets stuck. So offer to answer their questions too. Now you're not waiting on someone you never met. More on the two questions page.

04

Step 2: send the estimate the same day

Not next week. Today.

Right after the visit, they want it fixed. A week later, life got busy. They half forgot what you said. And the other guy who came out? Every day you wait, he gets a day to beat you.

So send it today, while they still care. Then text them that it's sent. Emails end up in spam.

05

Step 3: the next-day text

The next day, send one text. It asks one easy question. Say your name is Mike:

The next day

"Hi, it's Mike. Did the estimate come through okay?"

Easy to answer. And if it hit their spam, now you know.

06

Step 4: check in on their date

Remember the date they gave you? On that day, you check in.

On their date

"Hi, you said you hoped to start around now. Any questions I can help with?"

They told you to come back then. So it's not pushy.

07

Step 5: "Should I close this out?"

This one is my favorite. If they still go quiet, wait about a week. Then send five words:

About a week later

"Should I close this out?"

That's the whole text. Why does it work? It's easy to answer. And nobody likes to lose an option.

So a lot of the time, they write back, "No, wait. Let's book it." If they picked someone else, they tell you. Then you stop. More on the close-out text page.

08

One bonus: pick up when they call back

When they do call back, someone has to pick up. If you're on a roof, that call can go to the next guy.

That's why I like an AI receptionist that picks up when you can't. See how my clients' calls work.

Quick answers

How long should I wait to follow up on an estimate?

I tell my trade businesses to send it the same day. Text the next day with one easy question. Check in on the date they gave you. If they still go quiet, wait about a week. Then send "Should I close this out?"

Is it pushy to follow up on an estimate?

Not when they gave you the date. "You said you hoped to start around now" is just doing what they asked.

What if they picked someone else?

They usually tell you when you ask "Should I close this out?" Then you stop.
