How I make a small contractor look like the best choice in town (without lying)

Customers can't see your work before they call. They see your website. So on my clients' sites I use real photos. Never stock, never AI. I say every true claim loud. Licensed, insured, years, and five stars when it's really five stars. The first screen has the job and the town. It has a big call button and a real photo. And I add one thing that's only theirs. Nothing made up.

01

Real beats big

Big shops have big budgets. But a small shop has what they can't buy. A real owner, a real crew and real jobs.

So the goal isn't to look big. It's to look real, and look like the best choice. Without one made-up word.

02

1. Real photos

Not stock photos. The truck, the crew, and the job when it's done.

I never use AI pictures on a client's site. If the owner wants his face up there, great. If not, his work does the talking. More on the real photos page.

03

2. True claims, said loud

Licensed. Insured. Years in business. Five stars, when it's really five stars.

I never take a true claim off a client's site. If they earned it, people should see it. A customer can't see your work before they call. So the proof goes where they look first.

But if they can't prove it, it doesn't go on. No made-up awards. No "number one in town".

04

3. The first screen

People decide fast on a phone. So the proof sits right where they decide. Here's the order I use, top to bottom:

  1. The first screen: the job, the town, a big call button and a real photo. Right by the button goes a true fact or two. Like licensed and insured, or the star rating.
  2. Real job photos.
  3. The quote form, up high, not at the bottom.
  4. The reviews, word for word.

They land, they see proof, they tap call. That's the whole job of the top of the page.

05

4. One thing that's only theirs

Last, one thing that's only theirs. A detail from their trade or their town. So it doesn't look like every other contractor site.

06

The 5-second test

Here's a test for tonight.

  1. Open your site on your phone.
  2. Hand it to someone who doesn't know your shop.
  3. After five seconds, take it back.
  4. Ask them: what do we do, where, and how do you reach us?

If they miss one, fix the top of the page first.

07

Quick answers

What should be on the first screen of a contractor's website?

On my clients' sites, it's the job and the town. Then a big call button and a real photo. By the button goes a true fact or two. Like licensed and insured, or the star rating.

Where should the quote form go?

Up high, right after the real job photos. Not at the bottom of the page. Then the reviews, word for word.

Can I say I'm the best in town?

Not unless you can prove it. I never put made-up awards or "number one in town" on a client's site. True claims, said loud, work better.
