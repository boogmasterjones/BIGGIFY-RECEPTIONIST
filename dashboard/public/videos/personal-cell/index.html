Why my clients get a business line instead of giving out a personal cell

01

"Just call my cell"

I hear it on my calls all the time. "Just call my cell." It feels simple. It's the number you already have.

But here's what it costs you:

  • It rings at dinner. And on your day off. There's no "off".
  • You can't hand the call to anyone. It's your phone, in your pocket.
  • If you ever sell the business, the number goes with you, not with the shop.
02

The real cost: calls that ring out

The big one is this. When you're up on a roof, your cell just rings and rings.

A lot of people won't leave a message. They hang up and call the next shop. That job is gone, and you never even know it.

Try this tonight

Call your own cell from another phone. Don't pick up. Let it ring all the way out. That's exactly what your customers hear when you're busy.

03

What my clients get instead

My clients get one business line. It's for calls and texts.

  1. It rings the owner first. If he can take it, he takes it.
  2. If he can't pick up, my receptionist does. It gets the caller's name, number and what they need.
  3. The owner gets a text with all of that, so he can call back fast.
  4. Texts reach the right people, not just one guy's pocket.

The owner still talks to his customers. He just stops losing the ones who call while he's working.

04

Quick answers

Why not just forward my business line to my cell?

Then you're back to one phone in one pocket. If you're on a job, it still rings out. My clients' lines ring the owner first. Then the receptionist answers the calls he can't get.

What happens to texts on a business line?

On my clients' lines, texts reach the right people through my system. Not just one person's phone.
