02

Why social matters for a trade (and why it isn't about likes)

Someone finds you on Google, or a neighbor drops your name. Before they call, they tap your Facebook or Instagram to answer one question: is this a real, working business I can trust in my house?

A page with recent job photos, real faces and quick replies says yes. A page with one post from years ago says "maybe they closed". That's the whole job.

The honest truth about Google

Google has never said Facebook or Instagram posts raise your Maps ranking. Anyone selling social posts "to rank on Google" is selling a story. What social does: it backs up your Google profile, and keeping your name, phone and hours the same everywhere stops customers (and Google) from finding the wrong info.

03

Free sample: the 12-post rotation

"What do I post?" is why most trade pages go quiet. Fix it once: go down this list, one idea per post, then start again at the top. At two posts a week it takes six weeks to come round, and nobody notices the repeat because the jobs are different every time.

PostWhat to shoot or showWhy it works
1. A job photoOne clean, sharp photo of finished work. Tidy the area first.Proof you do the work, recently, near them.
2. Before and afterTwo photos from the same spot, same angle. Take the "before" before you touch anything.The most convincing post a trade can make.
3. The crewYou or your team, first names, on the job or by the truck. Ask each person first.People want to know who's coming to their house.
4. The truckYour lettered truck or van, parked at a job (no house numbers in shot).Says "real, established, local". Repeats your name and number.
5. A tipOne thing a homeowner can do or check themselves, with a photo of it.Shows you know your stuff and aren't just selling.
6. A review screenshotA real review, cropped to the stars and the words. First name only. Never edited.Someone else saying you're good beats you saying it.
7. A seasonal reminderThe job people should book before the season turns: storm prep, AC check, gutter clean, heater flush.Gives people a reason to call this week.
8. Behind the scenesLoading the truck at 6:30 am, the supply house, a training day, the shop.Shows you're busy and you care how it's done.
9. An FAQ answerThe question you get on every call ("Do you charge to come out?"), answered straight.Answers it before they ask, and filters out bad fits.
10. A community momentThe Little League team you sponsor, a local event you were really at, a thank-you to a supplier.Local people hire local people.
11. A new serviceSomething you really added: a new install type, a new town, weekend hours. Only if it's true.Tells past customers what else you can do.
12. A finished-job walk-through video20 to 40 seconds walking slowly through the finished job, phone upright and steady.Video is the hardest thing to fake, so it's the strongest proof.

Quick answers

Is it really free?

Yes. Enter your email and you can read and print the whole playbook straight away. There's nothing to buy and no call needed.

Do Facebook posts help me rank on Google Maps?

Google has never said that Facebook or Instagram posts raise your Maps ranking, so don't pay anyone who promises it. Social helps in other ways: people check you out there before they call, and your name, phone and hours matching everywhere keeps Google from showing wrong information.

How much time does it take?

About 10 minutes a week, plus taking three photos on each job. The playbook gives you the post ideas, captions and reply scripts so you aren't starting from a blank page.

Does this work for my trade?

Yes, if you do work at people's homes or businesses: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, tile, flooring, pressure washing, lawn, pest, pools and the like. Every post idea uses photos you already take on the job.
