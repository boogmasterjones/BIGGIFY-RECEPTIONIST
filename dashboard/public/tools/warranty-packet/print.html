1. Document checklist

Put all of this in one folder, on your phone and your computer. Requirements differ by network; this covers what they commonly ask.

Licenses

  • Trade license(s) for every state, county and city you'll work in
  • Business license or registration where required
  • Certifications you'll list (for example EPA refrigerant handling for HVAC techs)

Insurance

  • General liability certificate (COI) per company: named as certificate holder, and additional insured if asked
  • Your liability limits written down (some ask for a minimum per occurrence)
  • Commercial auto for every truck that goes to their customers
  • Workers comp certificate, or your state's exemption paperwork (ask each network if they accept it)

Tax and identity

  • W-9: legal business name and EIN, matching your bank account
  • Owner's photo ID
  • Bank details for direct deposit (enter them yourself in their secure portal, never by email)

People and trucks

  • List of techs who'll go into homes; each can pass a background check (some also check driving records)
  • Number of trucks, what you stock, brands you service
  • Photos of lettered trucks and uniforms, if you have them

Your numbers

  • Service area as a list of zip codes you'll really drive to
  • Hours, after-hours and emergency availability
  • How fast you'll call the homeowner back, and how soon you can usually be there
  • True cost per call and your lowest acceptable rate (page 5)

Before you sign anything

  • Their rate sheet for your trade
  • How they pay, and how many days after you invoice
  • Authorization rules: above what amount, and how (phone, portal)
  • Parts rules: who supplies, allowed markup
  • Workmanship warranty length they require from you
  • Any rules on contacting their members directly afterwards

3. COI request letter to your insurance agent

Email this to your agent. Send one request listing every company you're applying to, so the certificates come back together.

Subject: Certificates of insurance needed for home warranty networks

Hi [Agent's name],

I'm applying to work as a service contractor for the home warranty companies below. Each one needs a certificate of insurance before they'll send me work. Could you please issue a COI for each, showing my general liability, commercial auto and workers comp coverage?

For each company, please list them exactly as shown as certificate holder, and add them as additional insured on my general liability where marked:

Company (exact name and address as they gave it)Additional insured?Send to (their email/fax)

A few questions while you're in there:

  1. What are my current liability limits per occurrence and aggregate? Some of these companies have minimums.
  2. Does my policy allow additional insureds, and is there a cost to add them?
  3. Can you send updated certificates to these companies automatically when my policy renews?

Please email the certificates to me as well. Thanks for the help.

[Your name]
[Business name] · [Phone]

Tip: copy the certificate holder's name and address exactly from each company's instructions. A small mismatch is a common reason a certificate gets rejected.

Workers comp exemption?

If you're a one-person shop with a state exemption instead of a policy, ask each network before you apply whether they accept the exemption certificate. Some require a policy regardless. Write the answer in the tracker on page 4.

5. True cost per call worksheet

Compare the warranty rate with what a call actually costs you, not with your retail price. Use a typical call in your trade.

LineWhat to enterYours
ADrive time there and back (hours)
BTime on site, including diagnosis (hours)
CAuthorization calls, hold time, photos, portal and invoicing (hours)
DHours per call = A + B + C
ELoaded labor per hour: tech wage + payroll taxes + workers comp + benefits ($)
FOverhead per hour: monthly insurance, office, phone, software, etc. ÷ hours billed per month ($)
GLabor and overhead = D × (E + F) ($)
HMiles there and back × your cost per mile ($)
IOut of 10 calls, how many need a second trip?
JCost of a second trip: (A + hours on site for the return visit) × (E + F) + H ($)
KSecond-trip share = J × (I ÷ 10) ($)
LTRUE COST PER CALL = G + H + K ($)

What you'd actually get

MService fee you collect at the door, if your agreement has you collect it ($)
NWhat the company pays for a typical call in your trade, per their rate sheet ($)
OParts margin allowed on a typical call ($)
PYOU RECEIVE = what your agreement says you're paid in total (M, N and O, as their rules count them) ($)
QPROFIT PER CALL = P − L ($)
RDays until you're paid

Read it: if Q is negative on a typical call, every call loses money, no matter how many you get. If Q is small and R is long, warranty work only makes sense to fill hours you'd otherwise lose. Your lowest acceptable rate is the one that makes Q at least zero; write it on page 2.

Check how your agreement counts the service fee: some companies count it toward what they pay you rather than on top of it.

[Your name] · [Phone] · [Email]

3. Questions about rates and terms before signing

Subject: Questions on the [trade] agreement: [Business name]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for sending the agreement. Before I sign, could you confirm a few things?

  1. The rate for a typical [trade] diagnosis and repair in my area, and how the homeowner's service fee counts toward it.
  2. Above what amount I need authorization, and the fastest way to get it.
  3. Your parts rules: supplier, allowed markup, and how you handle parts on back order.
  4. How many days after I invoice you usually pay.
  5. How long a workmanship warranty you require from me.

Based on my costs, the lowest rate that works for us is [$ amount] for a typical call. Is there any room to get there?

Thanks,
[Your name] · [Phone]
