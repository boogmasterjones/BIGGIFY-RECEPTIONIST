Following up on a quote without being pushy

Most quotes that go quiet aren't a no. People get busy. One question at the walkthrough and two well-timed texts win a lot of them back. Here's the timing and the exact words.

01

At the walkthrough: ask when they're deciding

Most owners follow up on a random day, or never. Ask the customer, and they'll tell you exactly when to call.

Say this before you leave

"I'll have the quote to you by tomorrow. When are you hoping to decide? And is anyone else deciding with you? ... Great. Is it OK if I text you about it?"

  • The date is your follow-up day. Write it on the job, in your calendar or software.
  • The other person (a spouse, a landlord) is usually why quotes stall. Offer to answer their questions too.
  • The "OK to text?" gets you a yes on record, and texts get read.
02

Send the quote within 24 hours

The longer it takes, the colder they get, and the more time the next company has to beat you to it.

  • Put a "good until" date on it. Pick what's honest for your costs, like 30 days. It gives a reason to decide and protects you when material prices move.
  • Make it easy to read: what you'll do, one total, and how to say yes ("reply YES or sign here").
  • Email it, then text that it's sent. Quote emails land in spam and promotions folders all the time.
03

The follow-up timing

WhenWhat

ThenStop. If they come back, great.

That's two follow-ups. More than that starts to feel like pressure, and it's the kind of thing people mention in reviews.

04

Copy-paste texts

1. Quote sent

"Hi [first name], it's [your name] from [business]. I just emailed your quote for the [job]. It's good until [date]. If it's not in your inbox, check spam, or reply here and I'll resend it."

2. The day after their decision date

"Hi [first name], [your name] from [business]. You mentioned you'd be deciding on the [job] around now. Any questions I can answer? Happy to talk it through with [spouse's name] too."

3. A week later: close it out

"Hi [first name], should I close out your quote for the [job]? No problem either way. If the timing's just not right, I'm happy to check back whenever suits you."

The close-out text works because it's easy to answer. People who were putting it off often reply "no, wait, let's do it." People who went elsewhere will usually just tell you, and now you know.

When they reply

  • "Yes": "Great, thank you! I've got [day] or [day] open. Which works better?"
  • "We went with someone else": "Thanks for letting me know. Mind if I ask what made the difference? It helps me get better." Then leave it.
  • "Not yet": "No rush. When should I check back?" Write that date down. That's your new follow-up day.
Don't chase with a discount

Knocking money off in a follow-up teaches people to wait you out. Follow up to answer questions and make yes easy, not to lower the price.

05

Texting rules

  • Texting someone who asked you for a quote, about that quote, is fine. That's customer service, and they gave you their number for it.
  • It isn't permission for marketing. Don't add them to promotions or a mass text list without their written consent.
  • Say who you are in every text, and honor STOP (or "stop texting me") right away.
  • Text between 9 am and 8 pm their time.
  • Sending from software? Your number should be registered for business texting (called 10DLC). Ask your provider.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice.
