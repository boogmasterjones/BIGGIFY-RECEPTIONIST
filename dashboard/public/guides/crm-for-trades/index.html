01

Why most comparisons miss the point

Ask an owner why they bought their software and you'll hear "scheduling" and "invoicing". Ask what makes them money and you'll hear "the phone rang". The software that matters is the one that makes the phone ring again.

Every tool on this page can put a job on a calendar and send an invoice. Where they differ is the stuff that happens around the job:

  • Before the job: does a request from your website, Google or a phone call land in one place, tagged with where it came from?
  • After the estimate: does anyone follow up on the quotes that went quiet?
  • After the job: does the customer get asked for a review, put on a maintenance reminder, and hear from you again next season?

02

The 9 money features, and what to ask about each

Take this list into every demo. For each one, ask the same two questions: "Is it on the plan I'm being quoted?" and "Can you show me it running, not a slide?"

1. Automatic review requests after the job

The job gets marked done or paid, and the customer gets a text or email with your Google review link. No one has to remember. This is the cheapest steady source of new calls most trades have, because reviews feed your Google ranking and your close rate.

Ask: What triggers it (job finished or invoice paid)? Can I skip a customer who wasn't happy? Does it link straight to my Google review box? For the words to use, see reviews that stick.

2. Estimate follow-ups

Most unsold estimates aren't a "no". They're a "I got busy". An automatic text two days later and an email a week later wins jobs you already paid to quote.

Ask: Can I set the timing? Does it stop the moment the customer approves? Can it go by text and email?

3. Online booking, and your website form landing in the software

Customers who search at 10 pm want to book at 10 pm. Online booking lets them pick a time. A request form on your website does the same job for bigger work. Either way, the request should create the customer and the request inside your software, not an email someone has to retype.

For one of our clients, the website quote form creates the customer and the job request directly in Jobber, so nothing gets retyped or lost.

Ask: Is there an embed code for my own site, or only your hosted page? Can I limit bookable times so I don't get booked across town at 8 am? Does a booking ping my phone?

4. Customer financing

Offering monthly payments can turn a "we'll wait" on a big repair or replacement into a yes. In most of these tools a lending partner pays you up front and the customer pays them over time.

Ask: Who's the lender? What's my fee per financed job? (Jobber, for example, lists a 3.9% fee through its partner Wisetack. Fees differ by tool and plan.) Does the offer show right on the estimate?

5. Text messaging, with the registration done for you

Customers answer texts. "On my way", reminders, follow-ups and review asks all work better by text. But business texting in the US now needs carrier registration. More on that below, because it trips up a lot of owners.

6. Maintenance plans and recurring reminders

An HVAC tune-up, a water-heater flush, a quarterly pest visit, a yearly pool or roof check. Recurring work is booked revenue you don't have to market for. The software should create the next visit and remind the customer without anyone keeping a list.

Ask: Can it bill the plan automatically? Can it remind customers whose plan is about to lapse? Is this on my plan or only the top one?

7. Marketing campaigns to past customers

Your past customers already trust you. A short seasonal email ("hurricane season is coming, here's your generator check") goes to people who know your name. Built-in campaigns use the customer list you already have.

Ask: Email only, or text and postcards too? Can I send only to customers in one town or who had one type of job? Marketing texts need the customer's clear written OK first, so ask how the software records it.

8. Reporting on lead source

Without this, you're guessing which ad, directory or Google profile is paying for itself. Every request should carry a source (Google, website, referral, yard sign, Angi), and the report should show the jobs and dollars from each.

Ask: Is the source filled in automatically from my website and Google, or typed by hand? Does the report show revenue per source, not just lead counts?

9. Integrations: Google booking, call tracking, Zapier

  • Book on Google. Some tools can put a "Book online" button on your Google Business Profile that drops bookings into the software. It usually needs your address or service details to match exactly.
  • Call tracking. A tracking number on each ad or listing tells you which one made the phone ring. Some tools have it built in. Others connect to a call-tracking service like CallRail.
  • Zapier. A connector that links your software to thousands of other apps. It's how you fill the gaps, like sending a form from a website builder into your software.
03

Texting and 10DLC, in plain English

10DLC is how the US phone carriers vet businesses that text customers from a normal 10-digit number. Unregistered business texts can get filtered or blocked, and you'll never know the customer didn't get them.

  1. The software registers your business.It sends your legal business name, EIN, address and website to the carriers' registry. That's why it asks for your EIN.
  2. Your website has to look legit.Have your real business name, contact info and a privacy policy on the site. Some tools also ask you to publish their texting terms.
  3. Approval takes time.Jobber, for example, says registration can take up to 3 weeks. Housecall Pro may also ask for your EIN letter and a one-time code by text.
  4. Details must match exactly.Your legal name and EIN have to match your IRS paperwork. A mismatch is the most common reason registration fails.
Start the registration on day one

Even if you're still on a free trial elsewhere, find out what the software needs and gather it now: EIN letter, legal name exactly as the IRS has it, website with a privacy policy. Some tools don't allow two-way texting during a trial.

07

Which one for your size

This is our honest read, not a ranking. The right answer is the tool whose money features are on a plan you'll actually pay for and use.

Just you, a handful of jobs a week

  • A spreadsheet and Google Calendar is fine if every lead comes by phone and you're good at following up. Set a phone reminder to ask for a review after every job.
  • The moment you lose track of an estimate, move up. ServiceM8's free tier or Jobber's entry plan are low-risk first steps.

1 to 5 trucks, owner still in the field

  • Jobber or Housecall Pro fit most shops this size. Compare the plan that includes review requests, estimate follow-ups and two-way texting, not the cheapest plan on each.
  • Workiz is worth a look if your business lives on the phone and you want calls, texts and jobs in one place. Price out the phone add-on.
  • Kickserv if you want simple and cheap and you'll handle reviews and follow-ups another way.

5 to 15 trucks, maintenance plans matter

  • Housecall Pro's higher plans, FieldEdge or Workiz if service agreements are a big part of your revenue (HVAC, plumbing, pest, pools).
  • Make sure agreements, automatic billing and lapse reminders are on the plan you're quoted.

15+ technicians, a dispatcher or call center

  • ServiceTitan is built for this: memberships, call booking, marketing attribution. It's priced per technician and takes real setup time, so ask about setup fees and contract length before you sign.
08

Copy-paste follow-up texts

Whatever software you pick, these go straight into its estimate follow-up and review-request settings. Swap the brackets.

Estimate follow-up, day 2 (text)

"Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Just checking you got the estimate for the [job]. Happy to answer any questions or adjust it. Reply here or call [phone]."

Estimate follow-up, day 7 (email)

"Hi [First name], I know life gets busy. Your estimate for the [job] is still good, and I can get you on the schedule [this week / next week]. If the price is the sticking point, tell me and I'll see what we can do. [Your name], [Business], [phone]."

Review request, after the job (text)

"Thanks for having us out today, [First name]. If we did a good job, a quick Google review helps a small business like ours more than you'd think: [review link]. [Your name], [Business]."

Maintenance reminder (text)

"Hi [First name], it's been about a year since we [serviced your system]. Want me to get you on the schedule for a check-up? Reply with a day that works."

Keep it to two follow-ups on an estimate, then stop. One review ask, with one reminder at most. Never offer a discount or gift for a review; Google doesn't allow it.

09

The expensive mistakes

✕Costs you money

  • Paying for features you don't use. The top plan "just in case", a campaigns add-on that's never sent a campaign, a phone add-on next to the phone system you already pay for.
  • Not connecting the website form. The form emails someone, the email gets buried, the customer books the next company.
  • Leaving review requests off. Some tools ship with them on, some don't. Check.
  • Skipping lead source. Six months later you can't say which ad or listing paid for itself.

✓Do this instead

  • Every 90 days, list every plan and add-on. For each, write what money it brought in. No answer? Downgrade or cancel.
  • Send a test request from your own website and watch it land in the software, with the right source.
  • Mark a test job done and check your own phone for the review ask.
  • Make "where did you hear about us?" a required field on every form and phone booking.

Quick answers

What's the best CRM for a small trade business?

For most 1 to 5 truck shops, Jobber or Housecall Pro covers the features that bring in money: online booking, review requests, estimate follow-ups and texting. Pick the one whose money features are included in the plan you can afford, not the one with the longest feature list.

What's 10DLC and why does my software want my EIN?

10DLC is the US carriers' registration system for businesses that text customers from a normal 10-digit number. Unregistered business texts can be blocked. Software that texts for you has to register your business, which is why it asks for your legal name, EIN and website.

Is a spreadsheet and Google Calendar good enough?

It's fine when you're solo and every lead comes by phone. It stops being enough when you start losing track of estimates or forgetting to ask for reviews, because a spreadsheet never follows up on its own.

Should my website form go straight into my CRM?

Yes. A form that only sends an email gets missed, retyped or lost. Connect it so each request creates the customer and the request inside your software, and tag the lead source while you're at it.
