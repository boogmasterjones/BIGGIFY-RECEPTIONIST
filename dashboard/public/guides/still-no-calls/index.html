Getting calls

Everything's set up and the phone still doesn't ring?

Google profile, website, Facebook page. All done, and the phone sits there. Something is leaking, and it's usually one of a few things. This is the 15-minute check that finds it, in the order it's most often found.

13 min read Checked against Google's own ranking page By Wyatt, Biggify

You'll walk away with

  • A 15-minute tick-off check you can run on your phone today
  • A symptom table: what you're seeing, and what's usually causing it

The 20-second version

Jump to the 15-minute check →
  1. Search like a customer, not like you. "Plumber near me" on a phone, logged out. Searching your own name proves nothing.
  2. Check your main category. Too broad or wrong, and Google doesn't show you for the job people search.
  3. Count your reviews against the top 3 near you. Far behind means you're mostly invisible until you catch up.
  4. Test your own phone and form. Full voicemail, blocked unknown callers and broken forms lose calls you already earned.
  5. Ask every caller how they found you. Then you know what's working instead of guessing.

02

Can people find you? Search like a customer

The most common mistake: you search your business name, your profile pops up, and you figure you're "on Google". That only proves the profile exists. Nobody with a leaking water heater searches your name. They search the job.

  1. Use a phone, logged out.Open a private (incognito) window, or use a phone that isn't signed in to your Google account. Google shows you your own business more because you look at it a lot.
  2. Search two ways."[your trade] near me" (like "electrician near me") and "[service] [town]" (like "drain cleaning Mesa").
  3. Look at the map, not the ads.Skip the "Sponsored" results. The map with 3 businesses under it is where most local calls start. Note where you show, if at all.
  4. Search from where your customers are."Near me" uses the phone's location. Results at your house can be very different from results 10 miles away. Try it at a job across town.
  5. Write down the top 3.You'll compare yourself to them in the sections below.
What Google says it ranks on

Google's own help page names three things: relevance (do you match the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher) and prominence (how well known you are, including your reviews). It also says nobody can pay for a better spot on the map. Every leak below hits one of those three.

03

Your main category is wrong or too broad

Your main (primary) category tells Google what you are. It's one of the strongest things you control, and it's often wrong.

✓Pick this

  • The category that matches what customers search the most: "Plumber", "Roofing contractor", "Electrician", "Pressure washing service"
  • Extra (secondary) categories for the other work you really do, like "Water heater installation service"
  • Copy the top 3 near you: look at the category shown under their name

✕Costs you calls

  • Broad ones like "Contractor" or "Home improvement" when you're really a plumber
  • Your smallest side service as the main one
  • Categories for work you don't do. It doesn't help, and it can get reported

Changing it? Change it once, then give it a few weeks before you judge. Changing your main category is a bigger edit in Google's eyes, so don't change your name, phone or address the same week.

04

Where you are vs where you work

This one surprises owners the most. Google ranks you mostly from where your business is, not from the list of service areas you typed in.

  • Service areas don't move you. They tell customers where you'll drive. Adding 30 towns doesn't make you show up in all 30.
  • You'll show best near your address. If you work from home on the edge of the county, you'll show strongest on that edge. That's normal, not a bug.
  • To win a town farther out: give it its own page on your website (the work you've done there, real photos) and earn reviews from customers in that town.
  • Don't fake an address. PO boxes, mailbox stores and virtual offices get profiles removed. Use your real address and hide it if customers don't come to you.
Check the pin

Open your profile on Google Maps and look at where the pin sits. If it's off (wrong street, middle of a field), fix the address in your profile. A misplaced pin means Google measures your distance from the wrong spot.

05

Reviews: count, how recent, vs the top 3

Google says more reviews and good ratings help your local ranking. Customers use them too: two plumbers side by side, one with 6 reviews and one with 140, and most people tap the 140.

Brand-new business? Be honest with yourself

If you opened this year, you're up against companies with years of reviews. No setting changes that overnight. Fix the leaks in this guide so you don't lose the calls you do get, then build reviews job by job. It takes months, not days, and nobody can skip that part for you.

06

Is your profile actually finished?

Google says businesses with complete, accurate info are more likely to show up. "Set up" often means name and phone and nothing else.

  • Services: list every service you really do, in the words customers use. "AC repair", not "climate solutions".
  • Photos of real jobs: before-and-afters, your truck, your crew at work. Taken on your phone is fine. Stock photos look like everyone else.
  • Hours you really answer: if you show "Closed" at 6 pm, you get shown less at 6 pm. Longer hours only help if someone actually picks up.
  • Description: what you do, where, and who for, in plain words. No keyword lists.
  • Website link and phone: tap both from the profile on your phone. Make sure they go where they should.
  • Verified: an unverified profile won't show properly. If it says to verify, finish it.
07

Your website: four quick tests

Your Google profile links to your site, and plenty of customers check it before they call. Do these on your phone, not your computer.

  1. Does it say the trade and the town?The page title (the words on the browser tab and in Google results) and the big headline should both say what you do and where: "Plumber in Tulsa, OK". "Welcome to our website" tells Google and customers nothing.
  2. Does the phone number tap to call?Tap it. If nothing happens, or it's a picture of a number, you're losing the easiest call there is.
  3. Does the form actually work?Fill it in yourself with a test message. Did it reach your email or phone? Check spam too. A form can look perfect and send nowhere.
  4. Is it fast?Open it on cell data, not Wi-Fi. If you're waiting and waiting, so is the customer, and they'll hit back and call the next one. Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool will tell you what's slowing it down.

08

The calls you're already missing

Sometimes the phone did ring. You just never knew. Most people who hit voicemail don't leave a message; they call the next business on the list.

  • Voicemail full. Call yourself and check. A full box means the caller hears a dead end.
  • Unknown numbers silenced. Phones now have settings to silence or screen unknown callers, and carriers add spam filters. New customers are, by definition, unknown numbers. Check your phone's call settings and your blocked or spam call list.
  • Calls during jobs. You're on a roof or under a sink and can't pick up. Call back within minutes, not at the end of the day.
  • After hours. Evenings and weekends are when a lot of homeowners finally get around to calling. What does a 7 pm caller get?
  • The wrong number somewhere. An old number on the website, an ex-employee's cell on Facebook, a tracking line someone forgot to forward.
Do this tonight

Ask someone to call you from a number you've never saved, once during the day and once after hours. Listen to exactly what they hear. Then look at your missed-calls list for the last 2 weeks and count the numbers you never called back. Want to see what those cost? The free missed-call calculator does the math.

09

Can you tell where calls come from?

If you don't know which calls came from Google, the website or a neighbor's referral, you can't tell what's working. You'll keep paying for the wrong thing. Start simple:

  1. Ask every new caller "How did you find us?"Write it down on the job sheet or in your phone. One week of answers tells you more than most reports.
  2. Check your Google profile's numbers.In your Business Profile, open Performance. It shows calls and website clicks from your profile. If calls there go up but your phone doesn't ring more, look at section 08 again.

Later, a separate call-tracking number for your Google profile can count those calls for you. If you do that, follow the safe way to add it in our Google profile changes guide, so you don't trip a re-check.

10

What about Facebook?

Keep your Facebook page accurate (right phone, right hours, a few real photos) because some people check it. But for most trades, Facebook is rarely where the calls come from. Someone with a dead AC in July or water on the floor opens Google, not Facebook.

✓Better use of the same hour

  • Ask 3 recent customers for a Google review
  • Add 5 real job photos to your Google profile
  • Call back every missed call from this week
  • Test your website form and tap-to-call

✕Easy to sink time into

  • Daily posts to a page with few local followers
  • Boosting posts without knowing what a call costs you
  • Arguing in local groups

Local Facebook groups can bring work when a neighbor asks "anyone know a good roofer?" and someone tags you. That comes from doing good work and being known, more than from posting.

12

What to fix first this week

Don't do everything at once. Do it in this order: the first ones save calls you're already losing, the later ones bring in new ones.

  1. Stop the leaks (today, 20 minutes).Empty your voicemail, check unknown-caller and spam settings, test your form and tap-to-call. These are calls you already earned.
  2. Start asking "How did you find us?" (today).From now on, every new caller.
  3. Fix the main category (one change, then wait).The exact trade people search. Leave other big profile edits for next week.
  4. Start the review habit (this week, then forever).Every happy customer, same day, with a direct link.
  5. Finish the profile (an hour this week).Services, real job photos, true hours, description.
  6. Fix the website basics (when you can).Trade and town in the title and headline, speed, one page per town you want to win.
  7. Search like a customer again in a month.Same searches, same spots. Compare to the top 3 you wrote down.

Quick answers

Why do I show up for my name but not my service?

Searching your own name only proves your profile exists. Customers search the service and the town, and there Google picks by relevance, distance and prominence. A broad main category, a location far from the searcher, or fewer reviews than the businesses around you will push you down.

Will adding more service areas get me more calls?

Usually not. Service areas tell customers where you go, but Google ranks you mostly from where your business is. To win nearby towns, give each town a page on your website and earn reviews from customers there.

How long until a new business gets calls from Google?

There's no fixed number. A brand-new profile with a handful of reviews is up against businesses with years of reviews. Most owners see it build over months as reviews come in, not days. Fixing leaks like a wrong category or missed calls helps right away.

Is Facebook worth my time for trade calls?

Keep the page up to date because people check it, but most people with a leak or a dead AC search Google, not Facebook. Time spent getting reviews and real job photos on your Google profile usually brings more calls.
