## Real beats big {#real}

Big shops have big budgets. But a small shop has what they can't buy. A real owner, a real crew and real jobs.

So the goal isn't to look big. It's to look real, and look like the best choice. Without one made-up word.

## 1. Real photos {#photos}

Not stock photos. The truck, the crew, and the job when it's done.

I never use AI pictures on a client's site. If the owner wants his face up there, great. If not, his work does the talking. More on the [real photos page](/videos/real-photos).

## 2. True claims, said loud {#claims}

Licensed. Insured. Years in business. Five stars, when it's really five stars.

I never take a true claim off a client's site. If they earned it, people should see it. A customer can't see your work before they call, so the proof goes where they look first.

But if they can't prove it, it doesn't go on. No made-up awards. No "number one in town".

## 3. The first screen {#first-screen}

People decide fast on a phone. So the proof sits right where they decide. Here's the order I use, top to bottom:

1. **The first screen:** the job, the town, a big call button and a real photo. Right by the button, a true fact or two, like licensed and insured, or the star rating.
2. **Real job photos.**
3. **The quote form,** up high, not at the bottom.
4. **The reviews,** word for word.

They land, they see proof, they tap call. That's the whole job of the top of the page.

:::video best-choice-short-proof-button
:::

## 4. One thing that's only theirs {#only-theirs}

Last, one thing that's only theirs. A detail from their trade or their town. So it doesn't look like every other contractor site.

## The 5-second test {#test}

Here's a test for tonight.

1. **Open your site on your phone.**
2. **Hand it to someone who doesn't know your shop.**
3. **After five seconds, take it back.**
4. **Ask them:** what do we do, where, and how do you reach us?

If they miss one, fix the top of the page first.
