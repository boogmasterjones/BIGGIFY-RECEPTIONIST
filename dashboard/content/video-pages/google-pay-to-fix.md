## What I'd do, step by step {#steps}

Someone calls. They say they're Google. They say your profile has a problem, and they want money to fix it.

1. **Hang up.** Don't press any buttons. Don't "press 1 to speak to a specialist".
2. **Don't pay anything** and don't give out card or bank details.
3. **Log in to the profile yourself** and look. Is there really a problem?
4. **If it's suspended,** use Google's own appeal form. And never make a new profile.

## My rule of thumb {#rule}

If "Google" calls or texts me, I assume it's not Google.

Google emails me, from a real Google address. Not a stranger on the phone.

That's my own rule, from my own experience. Google's help page says real Google calls can happen. But here's what Google itself says about them:

> **What Google says** [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6212928) it will never ask for payment info over the phone, and it won't promise a special spot on Google. Hang up on calls that seem suspicious.

## If the profile really is suspended {#suspended}

[Google's appeal steps](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551):

- **Don't make a new profile.**
- **Fix the problem first,** then use Google's appeal form.
- **Send proof,** like a business license or a utility bill.
- **You only get one try,** so get it right the first time.

You don't need a stranger on the phone for any of that.
