## My 3 rules for any call I didn't make {#rules}

1. **Nothing private on the first call.** No sales numbers, no bank info, no tax papers, no card details.
2. **Look them up yourself.** Get the full company name. Find their real website on your own.
3. **Call back on a number you found,** not the one they gave you.

That last one is the same advice [Google gives](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6212928) for any call that seems off: reach the company through a number or website you trust, not the one in the message.

> **If it's real, it'll be real tomorrow** Anyone who says you have to decide on this call is a reason to hang up.

## "We can get you to page one of Google" {#page-one}

I get these calls all the time. I'd call them more spam than scam. They call a long list, and they don't know a thing about your shop.

**The tell:** they promise a spot. [Google's own page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo) on hiring an SEO says no one can guarantee a number one ranking on Google. It also warns about firms that email you out of the blue, or claim a "special relationship" with Google.

**What I do:** I ask one thing. "Show me the calls you got a shop like mine." Not clicks. Calls. [More on these calls](/videos/seo-calls).

## "I'd like to invest in your business" {#invest}

I get this call, and lots of contractors do too. It sounds great. But to me, it's usually a sales call, not a check.

**What I do:** I don't give out my sales, bank info or tax papers. I ask for the full company name, look them up myself, and call back on a number I find. [More on these calls](/videos/invest-calls).

## "This is Google. Pay us to fix your profile" {#google}

Someone calls, says they're Google, and wants money to fix your profile.

**What I do:** I hang up, and I don't press any buttons. My rule of thumb: if "Google" calls or texts me, I assume it's not Google. Google emails me, from a real Google address.

**What Google says:** it never asks for payment info over the phone, and it won't promise a special spot. Hang up on calls that seem suspicious.

Then I log in to the profile myself and look. If it's really suspended, I use Google's own appeal form, and I never make a new profile. [More on these calls](/videos/google-pay-to-fix).

## Who gets these calls {#who}

Any business with a phone number on Google. That's every trade owner reading this. You're not being picked on. You're on a list.

The best defense is boring: own your own stuff, know where you log in, and never decide anything on a call you didn't make.
