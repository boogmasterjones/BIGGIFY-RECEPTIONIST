# Biggify — Business Model & Product Overview

*Last updated: 2026-08-15*

---

## 1. What Biggify Is

**Biggify is a growth-and-operations partner for home-services businesses.** It works in two connected halves:

1. **Lead generation (the front door).** We get clients found by local customers through **SEO / local search** — ranking their Google Business Profile, maps, and website so the phone rings more. This is the familiar, easy-to-understand first sale, and it's sold as a monthly retainer.
2. **The automation platform (the upsell).** Once the phone is ringing, clients need to catch and handle every call: a **voice AI receptionist** that answers 24/7 and books appointments, plus a co-branded dashboard where they run their **entire workflow** — every job, appointment, contact, material, invoice, and notification in one place.

The two halves are deliberately linked. **The lead-gen manufactures the demand for the automation:** more calls create the obvious need for a receptionist to catch them and a workspace to run the extra jobs. And the automation makes the lead-gen *provable* — call tracking and closed-loop job attribution let us show exactly which SEO-driven calls turned into paying jobs, which is the one thing local SEO normally can't demonstrate.

The pitch is not "buy access to our software." It's: **"We bring you customers and give you the system to run them."** Each client's dashboard shows their own name and logo, exposes only the tools they pay for, and hides all the underlying infrastructure. To them, Biggify feels like bespoke software made for their trade.

**First real client:** an interior design firm. The platform is built general-purpose (works for any home-services trade — HVAC, plumbing, contractors, designers) but the first workflow template is tailored to interior design.

## 2. The Core Value Proposition

> **Biggify brings clients more customers and gives them the system to run those customers faster — so they grow the top line and the bottom line at the same time.**

There are three levers, and they reinforce each other:

1. **More leads (growth).** Local SEO gets the client found by customers actively searching for their service. This is the demand engine — it fills the top of the funnel.
2. **Never miss a lead (capture).** The AI receptionist answers every call 24/7 and books it, so none of the new demand leaks out through voicemail or missed calls.
3. **Run the whole job in one place (efficiency).** Instead of juggling texts, spreadsheets, email threads, sticky notes, and vendor tabs, everything about a job lives in one workspace that updates itself. The efficiency thesis: cut job-completion time by ~25% and a client can take on ~25% more jobs with the same team.

**Why the combination matters more than any single lever:** the lead-gen creates the demand that makes the automation obviously necessary, and the automation produces the attribution data that proves the lead-gen is working. Each half fixes the other half's weakness (see §7).

## 3. The Problem We Solve

Home-services businesses (especially small ones) lose money and time in predictable ways:

- **Not enough leads.** Customers search Google/Maps for a service and call whoever shows up first. If the business isn't ranking locally, it never even gets the chance.
- **Missed calls = missed jobs.** Owners are on a job site, not by the phone. Every unanswered call is a potential customer calling the next company on the list.
- **Work is scattered.** Job details live across text messages, email, paper, spreadsheets, and the owner's head. Nothing is in one place, so time is wasted hunting for information and re-entering it.
- **Sourcing is manual and slow.** Finding materials, tracking prices, saving product links, and following orders/shipping is a tab-juggling chore.
- **Admin eats billable time.** Invoicing, payroll hours, and reporting are done by hand at night and on weekends.
- **Tools don't talk to each other.** A calendar app, a notes app, a quoting tool, and QuickBooks that all live in separate silos.

Biggify collapses all of that into one connected system that a non-technical owner can actually use.

## 5. How the Business Model Works

### Land with lead-gen, expand into automation
The go-to-market is a **two-step, land-and-expand** motion:

1. **Land** with a **local-SEO retainer** — a service every owner already understands ("get me found on Google"). It's an easy first sale, it's recurring, and it's the demand engine.
2. **Expand** by upselling the **automation platform** early — the receptionist to catch the new call volume, then the workspace to run the extra jobs, then invoicing/payroll/stats as they grow.

Marketing leads with lead-gen; automation is the near-term upsell that deepens the account and locks it in.

### Two revenue axes
| Axis | What it is | Pricing |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead-gen retainer** | Local SEO — the recurring core and the front door | Monthly retainer ($299–$2,500/mo by tier) |
| **Automation subscription** | Receptionist + job workspace + modules | Monthly SaaS; **bundled/discounted** when taken with a retainer |

The retainer is the revenue; the automation is the **retention-and-proof engine**. Because software has near-zero marginal cost, automation is bundled cheap (or included at higher tiers) to maximize stickiness, attribution data, and the upsell path — **the rule is to discount the software, never the SEO.**

### Packages
Pricing is anchored on Van Westendorp analysis: local SEO for a small home-services business has an acceptable band of ~$355–$545/mo (sweet spot ~$435–455); the automation platform bands at ~$130–$226 (indifference ~$190). SEO only *becomes* a $750+ product when it's bundled with the automation that **proves it works** — so higher tiers are always SEO + automation, never SEO alone.

| Package | SEO / mo | Automation | Total / mo | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** (hybrid) | $299 base **+ ~$40 per booked appointment** | Receptionist included (it tracks bookings) | ~$299 + results | Solo operators, cash-tight, or skeptics who want to pay for proof |
| **Local** (SEO entry) | $449 | — (add for $140) | $449 | Small established business wanting to get found — the mass-market entry |
| **Growth** (bundle) | $749 | +$140 | **$889** | Established business that wants leads *and* the system to run them |
| **Dominate** (bundle) | $1,500–2,500 | Included | $1,500–2,500 | Competitive metros / multi-location |
| **Automation Only** | — | $219 | $219 | Wants the software, not the SEO |

**Bundle economics:** automation is $219 standalone but **+$140** when added to any SEO tier (a $79 hook), and included free at Dominate. The $219 standalone is priced at the top of its band as a mild decoy so the bundled +$140 always reads as the better deal.

### SEO deliverable ladder (what justifies each tier)
The price gap between Local and Growth is justified on **delivery**, not just on price — Growth ranks the client in more places, faster, with active off-page work. The two differences an owner instantly understands are **geography** (more towns) and **speed** (ranks faster).

| Deliverable | Local ($449) | Growth SEO ($749) | Dominate ($1,500–2,500) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Service areas** | 1 city/town | 3–5 towns | Full metro / multi-location |
| **Keywords / services** | Core services only | Full service line + long-tail | Exhaustive + competitive terms |
| **Content cadence** | 1–2 pages/mo | 4–6 pages/mo | 8+ pages/mo |
| **Off-page (links + citations)** | Foundational | Active ongoing campaign | Aggressive authority building |
| **Review generation** | Basic request setup | Managed campaign | Managed + reputation strategy |
| **Reporting** | Monthly summary | + attribution loop (via automation) | + attribution + competitor tracking |

*(Design choice: Local stays SEO-only with a monthly summary; the automation-powered attribution loop is a Growth-and-up feature, so proof is itself an upgrade incentive.)*

Later, an **outcome-based option** (per booked appointment, tracked automatically by the receptionist — already the model in Starter) can extend to any tier once attribution is trusted — a "you only pay when we book you a job" wedge.

### Co-branded, feature-gated
- **Co-branded:** each client sees their own business name and logo. No Biggify/vendor branding is pushed on their customers.
- **Feature-gated by plan:** every business has an entitlement map that controls which modules it can access. A client on a lower tier simply doesn't see the modules they haven't bought — so the product always feels tailored, never "locked."

### Onboarding today → self-serve later
- **Now:** Biggify onboards each client by hand (white-glove setup — configure their business, services, phone number, stages, and template).
- **Later:** self-serve signup so businesses can spin up their own dashboard.

### Where revenue comes from
- **Lead-gen retainers** — the primary recurring line and the acquisition wedge.
- **Automation subscriptions** — bundled with retainers (retention) or sold standalone.
- **Expansion revenue** as clients turn on more modules (invoicing, team/payroll, statistics, integrations).
- **Usage-linked** elements where relevant (call volume, SMS); **outcome-based** per-booked-appointment fees later.
- Payment processing runs through **Stripe** (Stripe Billing for subscriptions; Stripe Connect later so clients can collect payment from *their* customers through Biggify).

### Open question to resolve
**Who owns the ranking asset?** Ranking the client's *own* Google Business Profile + website is cleaner and more honest but lets them leave with the rankings; driving through a site/number *Biggify* owns is stickier but can feel like hostage-taking. This choice sets both churn risk and how the exit conversation goes — decide it before the pricing page ships.

## 6. The Product — Feature by Feature

Everything is organized around one idea: **a Job is the center of the workspace.** Appointments come second. Everything else supports jobs.

### 6.1 AI Receptionist (live today)
A voice AI that answers the business's phone line.
- Takes calls 24/7, speaks naturally, and never sends callers to voicemail.
- **Books appointments**, answers questions about services and service area, and **gathers caller info** (name, number, what they need).
- Feeds everything it learns straight into the dashboard — new callers become contacts, new requests become jobs/appointments.
- Runs on the business's own phone number.

### 6.2 The Job Workspace (the heart of the product)
Click into any job and you get its **entire world** on one page — not a status row, but a living workspace:
- **Process bar / stages:** the full lifecycle of the job as customizable stages (e.g., for interior design: Discovery → Concept → Sourcing → Presentation → Ordering → Install → Reveal). Stages advance manually or automatically. **Each client defines its own pipeline.**
- **Tasks:** checklists that update both automatically and manually, so nothing falls through the cracks.
- **Materials & sourcing:** save products to the job, with vendor, price, image, SKU, and dimensions.
- **AI material autofill:** paste a product link → the AI crawls the page and auto-fills the name, vendor, price, image, SKU, and dimensions. No manual data entry.
- **Files & photos:** upload plans, site photos, and design assets to the job *(building next)*.
- **Activity & notes:** a running record of what's happening on the job.

### 6.3 Rooms & Planning — the "Biggify Board" *(next build)*
For room-based work (interior design, remodels), the job is organized by **room**:
- Arrange rooms, each with **square footage, measurements, pricing/budget, and notes**.
- Materials and photos attach to a specific room.
- Budget vs. actual rolls up from rooms to the whole job.
- **Pinterest import** *(later)* — if the API allows, let clients pull in inspiration boards directly.

### 6.4 Sourcing *(extends the AI autofill)*
- Auto-crawl vendor directories and **save materials to the job**.
- **Compare pricing** across sources.
- Once ordered, **track shipping** — all inside Biggify, no more chasing order emails.

### 6.5 Appointments & Calendar *(after the workspace)*
- Native in-platform calendar for scheduling.
- **Two-way Google Calendar sync**, so the business's existing calendar stays in lockstep.
- The AI receptionist books directly into it.

### 6.6 Contacts (live today)
- Full customer directory with tags and history.
- Populated both manually and automatically (AI-captured callers land here).

### 6.7 Automations & Slack *(after the workspace)*
- Jobs **auto-update** their tasks and stages as work progresses.
- **Slack integration** so the team communicates about jobs in Slack instead of scattered email — cutting internal email overhead.

### 6.8 Invoicing *(later phase)*
- Auto-generate invoices with direct access to all the job's data (materials, rooms, hours).
- Powered by Stripe; eventually lets clients collect payment from their own customers.

### 6.9 Team & Payroll *(later phase)*
- **Hour and pay tracking** per team member.
- Ready-made **payroll reports** so running payroll is a click, not a night's work.

### 6.10 Data Import *(later phase)*
- Import existing data from **QuickBooks, Excel**, and similar tools, so a new client can move in without starting from scratch.

### 6.11 Notifications
- In-app, email, and SMS notifications, with **per-user preferences** so each team member controls what they get and how.

### 6.12 Statistics *(final phase)*
- Business analytics built on top of the complete, connected dataset — job throughput, revenue, cycle times — so owners can see exactly where time and money go.

## 7. Why This Wins

- **Growth + operations in one partner.** No SEO agency can prove which calls closed, and no software tool supplies the calls. Biggify does both — owning the funnel from "found on Google" to "paid invoice."
- **The two halves fix each other's weakness.** Local SEO is slow (3–6 months to rank) and hard to prove; the automation delivers *immediate* value (never miss a call, organized jobs) to bridge the wait, and its call-tracking + closed-loop attribution finally proves the SEO is working. In return, the SEO feeds the automation the call volume that makes the workspace worth having.
- **The upsell writes itself.** More calls create the obvious need for a receptionist to catch them and a workspace to run the extra jobs — so the automation is the natural next step, not a cold pitch.
- **Land with the familiar, expand into the novel.** "Get me found on Google" is an easy first sale; once trust is built, expanding into AI receptionist + workspace is low-friction.
- **Provable ROI, automatically.** "We sent 40 leads, booked 22, you closed 9 worth $47K" — a monthly, per-client proof point that a made-up efficiency number could never match.
- **Feels bespoke, scales like SaaS.** Co-branding and feature-gating make each client feel like they got custom software, while Biggify runs one platform under the hood.
- **Sticky by design.** A client embedded in both their marketing *and* their operations, with the data to prove both work, has little reason to leave.

## 8. Build Roadmap

### Product (the automation platform)
1. ✅ **AI receptionist** — live (takes calls, books, captures leads).
2. ✅ **Multi-tenant foundation** — dashboard, auth, per-client data isolation, feature entitlements, co-branding.
3. ✅ **Contacts** + **Jobs** CRUD.
4. ✅ **Job workspace core** — process bar (customizable stages), tasks, materials with AI paste-a-link autofill.
5. ⏭ **Rooms / planning layer** — rooms with footage, measurements, budget; materials per room.
6. ⏭ **Files & photos** upload on jobs.
7. **Appointments + calendar** (native + two-way Google sync).
8. **Automations + Slack.**
9. **Invoicing** (Stripe).
10. **Team & payroll.**
11. **Data import** (QuickBooks, Excel).
12. **Statistics.**

Throughout: wire the AI receptionist into the shared database so calls automatically create contacts, jobs, and appointments; and move onboarding from white-glove toward self-serve.

### Lead-gen + attribution (what makes it a growth partner)
- **Local SEO delivery** — the retainer service that fills the funnel (GBP optimization, local content, citations, reviews). Front door and primary revenue.
- **Call tracking → attribution** — tie SEO-driven calls to the receptionist and then to closed jobs in the workspace, producing the monthly "leads → booked → closed → $" proof report. This is the piece that makes the SEO provable and the two products reinforce each other.
- **Outcome-based billing (later)** — once attribution is trusted, offer per-booked-appointment pricing as an even lower-risk wedge.

### Decisions still open
- **Who owns the ranking asset** (client's GBP/site vs. a Biggify-owned property) — sets churn risk and exit dynamics.
- **Final price points** within the illustrative ranges, ideally validated with a real Van Westendorp + purchase-intent survey on prospects.
